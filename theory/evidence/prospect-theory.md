# Prospect Theory

**Sources:**
- Kahneman, D. & Tversky, A. (1979). "Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk." *Econometrica*, 47(2), 263–292.
- Tversky, A. & Kahneman, D. (1992). "Advances in Prospect Theory: Cumulative Representation of Uncertainty." *Journal of Risk and Uncertainty*, 5(4), 297–323.
- Samuelson, W. & Zeckhauser, R. (1988). "Status Quo Bias in Decision Making." *Journal of Risk and Uncertainty*, 1(1), 7–59.
- CEB/Google/Motista. "From Promotion to Emotion: Connecting B2B Customers to Brands."
- *Journal of Economic Psychology* (2024). "A meta-analysis of loss aversion in risky contexts." (Validates λ ≈ 2.25 across 30+ studies.)

**Abstract.** Prospect Theory shows that human decision-making under risk deviates systematically from rational utility theory: outcomes are evaluated relative to a reference point (typically the status quo), not as absolute wealth states. The value function exhibits *loss aversion* — losses loom roughly 2.25× heavier than equivalent gains. Applied to B2B sales, this predicts that Status Quo Bias makes "No Decision" the dominant competitor, and that reducing perceived risk yields ~2.25× more purchase motivation than equivalent ROI gain. Personal Value (career safety) drives commercial outcomes at twice the rate of Business Value (ROI metrics).

**Key claims:**
- Reference dependence: outcomes are evaluated against the status quo, not in absolute terms.
- Loss aversion (λ ≈ 2.25): losses are weighted ~2.25× as heavily as gains in decision calculus.
- Diminishing sensitivity (α, β ≈ 0.88): each additional unit of gain or loss has less marginal impact.
- Status Quo Bias dominates B2B; "No Decision" is the primary competitor, not rival vendors.
- Personal Value (career, reputation, confidence) has 2× the impact on closure as Business Value (ROI).
- Re-anchoring the reference point via Cost of Inaction converts the "Safe No" into a loss frame.

**Supports in TCG:**
- **Axiom III — Law of Uncertainty Inflation** — explains *why* $\Delta_A$ multiplies friction rather than reducing value. Asymmetry inflates the perceived downside via loss aversion. This is represented by the transaction cost equation $y = a\hat{\Delta}_A^2 + c$, where the risk aversion coefficient $a$ is anchored to the loss aversion coefficient ($\lambda \approx 2.25$). The anchor is an analogy and not an inheritance: $\lambda$ is measured and dimensionless, and $a$ is unfitted and carries units of annual contract value.
- **Friction Allocation Principles** — risk-mitigation tactics (fixed-price scope, paid pilots) are how friction is structured to address loss aversion.
- **Decay Clock** — value decay over time corresponds to the buyer's reference point shifting back toward status quo as urgency fades.
- **Akerlof Exit Threshold** — loss aversion + status quo bias explain why buyers tolerate suboptimal incumbents rather than switching.
