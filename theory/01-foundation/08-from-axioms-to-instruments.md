# From Axioms to Instruments

**Version:** 1.0
**Purpose:** To derive, from the three axioms and the four definitions, what a reader must be able to do and in what order: determine the state of the three transaction costs, plan their minimization, and execute the plan with the buyer. This file names activities and outcomes. It names no tool. The instruments are built from it and trace to it, and nothing here is inherited from the practice it will replace.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Everything in this file is a consequence.** Each section states which axiom or definition it follows from. A row in the activity grid, a column in a ledger or a rule about who reads what that cannot be traced back is a mistake, and section 7 records what is asserted rather than derived.

---

## 1. Three phases, two levels

The [Constitution](./00-tcg-constitution.md) states each axiom at the level where a seller meets it and says which decision each produces. Read in order, the three decisions are the three things a reader has to be able to do.

| Phase | What the reader must be able to do | From | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Determine the state** | Read which cost dominates and how long the vector is, first for a market and then for a deal | Axiom I at the market, Axioms II and III at the deal | Market, then deal |
| **Plan the minimization** | Choose which gaps to close, in what order, with what evidence, at what spend to the seller, and how much of it before signature | Axioms II and III together | Deal, bounded by the market reading |
| **Execute with the buyer** | Run the activities the plan orders and re-read the direction at every boundary | Axiom I selects, Axiom III closes, Axiom II stages | Deal |

**Diagnosis has two levels because the axioms do.** Axiom I is stated at the market, and its decision, which instruments to run, is a decision about what an organization builds and staffs. That decision is taken before any deal is met. Axiom II's gate and Axiom III's gaps are stated at the workflow and the deal, and they flex the apparatus around the motion the market reading chose. So the market reading comes first and the deal reading varies around it. A book of deal readings that disagrees with its market reading is the check on the market reading, and the disagreement is worth more than either number.

**The plan is the phase the framework has never had.** Every earlier instrument read a deal and then jumped to an instrument set. Nothing between them said what the seller was trying to make true, or what it would cost them to do it. Section 6 supplies it, and it turns out to be a reading of the other instruments rather than an instrument of its own.

## 2. Determining the state

### 2.1 The market reading

A market is the buyers who call a problem by one name, and Axiom I is read across that population coarsely on purpose. What can be counted about a population before any buyer is met, by component:

| Component | What is countable about the population | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| **Search** | Whether buyers can name the problem, how many alternatives they enumerate, whether published material lets them compare, and whether this seller has a path to them | How much of the buyer's search cannot be resolved without a seller present |
| **Bargaining** (the field's *consensus*) | The seats the reference workflow carries at the typical buyer, and whether a formal body sits over them | How large the coalition is before anyone is met |
| **Enforcement** (the field's *implementation*) | Who codified the workflow, a regulator or each buyer, how deep the integration surface runs, and how far the typical buyer diverges from the reference | How specific the investment is, and whether the population converges or fragments |

The reading emits a direction and a level for the population, and the direction is the motion the organization must build. It then emits a second thing the buyer's vector cannot: **the seller's cost to serve, by component.** By the cost-to-serve corollary of Axiom I, the seller pays the mirror of each cost, so the same buyer vector costs two differently built sellers different amounts. The amortizability corollary of Axiom II then closes the reading: can this population's contract value and frequency recover the spend its vector demands from this seller. Pursue, restructure, or decline.

The organization-level failure this reading prevents is the one the Constitution names as mis-composed at the organization: a motion adopted because it worked for a seller in a different market, aimed at a cost this market's buyers do not carry.

**The reading accumulates, and its fill is the seller's differentiation.** What can be counted about a population is not fixed. It is a ledger the seller fills from every deal reading that feeds it, and how much of it is filled is how much unique insight the seller holds about that market. [04-seller-surplus-model.md](./04-seller-surplus-model.md) section 7.5 carries the seller's side. Three consequences follow for the reading itself. It emits a fourth quantity, the unfilled fraction, which is the seller's own market-level ignorance and the honest measure of how far to trust the other three. The cost to serve rises with the unfilled fraction, because every Map cell is being filled for the first time. And an entrant whose ledger is thin cannot pull Axiom III's third lever cheaply, closing a gap, so they are pushed onto the two weaker ones, price and risk, until the ledger fills. That is why an entrant prices below an entrenched competitor, and the framework's conjecture that the third lever moves the most surplus is why the entrant's margin is thin. The floor is the entry rule: a seller with an empty ledger has no reading to act on and should not be in the market.

The unfilled fraction is a seller cost on all three columns. It reaches the buyer's cost through the enforcement pair alone, where the seller's ignorance is half the gap, and the buyer's search and bargaining gaps still have no seller side.

### 2.2 The deal reading

A deal is one buyer's divergence from the reference workflow, with the seats their version carries. The deal reading emits the quantities the Constitution names: the level on base cost, which is specificity and which discovery does not move, the direction after amplification, which moves at every boundary, the frequency, and the three gaps.

Each gap is its own pair's uncertainty about its own outcome, per Axiom III, and this file states the bargaining gap at the level of the claim so that no instrument can inherit the older proxy.

- **The search gap** is the buyer's, about the market. It is the share of what the buyer needs in order to rank the alternatives that they do not yet hold as evidence.
- **The bargaining gap** is each seat's, about its own outcome. It is the share of seats whose occupant has not stated, on the record and with a definite sign, what the change does to the thing they are measured on. A seat the seller has mapped but whose occupant has not confirmed is an open gap, however good the seller's map. A stated hedge is the gap observed directly.
- **The enforcement gap** is bilateral, the seller's about the buyer's environment and the buyer's about the seller's capability, and it is the one pair whose two halves are read separately and summed.

### 2.3 Definition works on the gaps

*This subsection is a conjecture. It follows from the axioms and nothing has tested it.*

A codified workflow lets the buyer name what they need, lets the seller map it, and lets each seat state what the change does to it. So definition lowers all three gaps. It does not lower the base costs, and it raises two of them. A workflow is codified because people are measured on it, so a change to it touches more seats and produces more casualties, and the bargaining base cost rises. A codified workflow is an embedded one, so the buyer's own adaptation is larger, and the enforcement base cost rises. Only the search base cost can fall, and only when codification converges across buyers, which is what a regulator produces and a single buyer does not.

Definition is therefore a verifiability variable and belongs to Axiom III. A mature, codified deal is easier to run and no cheaper to buy. *Falsifier:* a book of deals in which workflow maturity predicts lower base cost with size held constant.

## 3. What an instrument is

The three axioms constrain an instrument in three ways, and together they define one.

1. **Axiom I indexes it.** An instrument works on one component. A motion is the set of instruments for the component that binds, and an instrument aimed at a component that is not binding leaves the deal where it was.
2. **Axiom II sizes and times it.** The level says how much apparatus the deal can carry. The second clause says how much of the cost must be paid before signature, so an instrument set is sequenced and front-loaded in proportion to specificity. Below the boundary the set is empty.
3. **Axiom III says what it produces.** Evidence that closes a named pair's uncertainty about their own outcome. The evidence is a signal, and by the Friction Allocation Principles it counts only if it cannot be automated away, is borne by the party making the claim, scales with the size of the claim, and is adjudicated by someone who loses when it is wrong. Fail one and the instrument is cheap talk.

**Two classes.** Most instruments close a gap. A second class lowers a base cost, and the framework has always had members of it without naming the class: restructuring a one-shot sale as a subscription lowers the cost of governing specificity, reducing incentive variance in the coalition lowers the bargaining base cost, and designing the product closer to the workflow a population runs lowers divergence. The plan in section 6 covers both classes, because a workshop that gets a committee to state its trade-offs aloud is lowering variance, and calling that a gap-closer misdescribes what it does.

**Five verbs.** An instrument does one of five things to a cost, and each verb is defined the same way whichever component it acts on.

| Verb | What it does | Traces to |
|---|---|---|
| **Read** | Counts what can be seen from outside a population or a deal | Axiom I's direction, Axiom II's level |
| **Map** | Learns what is inside one buyer, and closes the gap on the side that can be closed by asking | Axiom III |
| **Test** | Produces the costly signal that closes the gap on the side that can only be closed by proof | Axiom III and the Friction Allocation Principles |
| **Stage** | Reallocates exposure and control so that one irreversible decision becomes a sequence | Axiom II's governance and staged-commitment corollaries |
| **Maintain** | Re-earns what drift erodes, at the rate each component's gap rebuilds | Axiom III's second clause |

Read and Map differ by level. Map and Test close gaps. Stage and Maintain do something else, and a reader who treats the five as five kinds of proof will build the wrong instruments for the last two.

## 4. The activity grid

Five verbs on three costs. Each cell is an activity and the outcome it works toward. No cell names a tool.

| | **Search** (buyer against the market) | **Bargaining** (each seat against its own outcome) | **Enforcement** (seller and buyer against each other) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Read** | Whether this buyer population can name the problem, enumerate its alternatives, compare them on published material, and be reached by this seller at all. Outcome: how much of the buyer's search cannot be resolved without a seller present. | The seats the workflow carries, how many can say no, whether a formal body sits over them, and how many occupants have stated their own exposure. Outcome: the size of the coalition and the share of it whose outcome is known to itself. | How far the buyer's workflow diverges from the reference, how many systems exchange data, how many procedures change, how many exception paths are undocumented, and how much of that either side has evidence for. Outcome: the specificity of the investment and both halves of the gap. |
| **Map** | Define what the buyer is choosing between: the problem's boundary, the alternative set including build and do nothing, and the reference workflow the product speaks to. Outcome: the buyer can name the problem and locate the product inside the set. | For each seat, what the change does to the thing its occupant is measured on: budget, headcount, standing, control. Sign and size, including the casualty. Outcome: every seat named with its exposure, and the seller knows who loses. | The buyer's environment and workflow as it is: exception paths with volumes, integration points with schemas, the political map of who loses control. Outcome: seller ignorance closed, and the divergence from the reference known step by step. |
| **Test** | Verifiable proof that travels without the seller: third-party comparison, reference architectures, produced outcomes a buyer can check alone. Outcome: the buyer ranks alternatives on evidence rather than claims. | Each occupant confirms their exposure on the record, and the objections are surfaced in a room built to hear them. Outcome: stated positions replaced by confirmed exposures, and variance visible rather than suppressed. | The seller proves capability in the buyer's environment at the seller's cost, and the buyer's skeptic attacks the plan before signature. Outcome: buyer uncertainty closed, product limits acknowledged in writing, failure modes named with owners. |
| **Stage** | A path to the buyer that carries a stake: a channel, partner or consortium that loses something when a bad match passes. Where specificity is low, a trial that hands fit verification to the buyer. Outcome: the buyer can reach the seller, and whoever adjudicates between them bears consequences. | Written decision criteria and a decision sequence the coalition agrees to before the vendor decision, with the casualty's containment written in. Outcome: the coalition's decision is a sequence with owners rather than a late veto. | Mutual commitments sequenced into gates, each with a priced right to stop, and residual control allocated for the states nobody specified. Outcome: one irreversible decision becomes a series, and exposure is staged for whoever sinks the specific investment. |
| **Maintain** | Keep the category from re-pooling: refresh the verifiable material as entrants arrive and the name drifts, and watch whether cheap signals are swamping costly ones. Outcome: the population can still rank, and the seller's rank is re-earned. | Re-read the seats at every occupant change, reorganization and budget cycle. Outcome: a new occupant states their exposure before the gap reopens into a stall, and a champion's departure is a seat event rather than a surprise. | Re-map the environment as staff, systems and workflow change, and re-earn credibility with delivered outcomes. Outcome: the gap stays closed, and the next renewal compares a mapped incumbent against an unmapped challenger. |

**Three observations from filling the grid.**

- **Every cell below Read is a seller cost.** The column of cells a market's direction points at is that market's cost to serve, and the cells a deal's direction points at are that deal's.
- **Test collapses toward Map on the bargaining column.** An occupant stating their own exposure is the map and the confirmation in one act, and what remains distinct is the collective version, the room in which the objections are heard together. The row is kept because the room is a distinct activity, and section 7 records this as the weakest split in the grid.
- **Stage is where the plan meets governance.** On every column it is the cell that converts a decision into a sequence with owners, which is why it cannot be run before Map and Test have said what the sequence must protect.

## 5. Three ledgers on one spine

The grid says what to do. Axiom III says who must be in the room when it is done, because each cost runs between its own pair of parties. That gives three documents rather than one, each keyed to the unit its pair is about, and each growing to the right as the deal proceeds.

| | **Search** | **Bargaining** | **Enforcement** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pair** | Buyer against the market | Each seat against its own outcome | Seller and buyer against each other |
| **Row** | An alternative the buyer must rule out, including build and do nothing | A seat | A workflow step |
| **Lives at** | Market level. One per market, written before any deal, reused by every deal in it | Deal level. One per deal | Deal level. One per deal |
| **Filled by** | The seller publishes it. The buyer can complete it alone, which is the point | The seller with each occupant, one at a time | Both parties, in the open |
| **Done when** | The buyer has placed the product in the set on evidence and can reach the seller | Every seat has stated its exposure, and every loss is contained in writing | Every row matches the reference or carries a gated mitigation with an owner |

**The spine.** The reference workflow, written once by the seller, is the row key of the enforcement ledger and the index of the other two. Each alternative in the search ledger is compared on which steps it covers. Each seat in the bargaining ledger is defined by which steps it runs, owns, pays for or polices. The three cross-reference by step without merging, and a change to the reference propagates to all three. A seller who has not written the reference workflow down cannot open any of the three, which is Axiom II's corollary that a product must name its workflow, applied before selling begins.

**How the spine is keyed.** Two workflows share a step only if the steps are typed the same way, so the library the reference workflows are drawn from needs a typing. A reference workflow has two kinds of step: internal steps the product performs alone, and touchpoints where it meets the buyer's system of record. Touchpoints come in five types, and the types hold in any vertical. Enrollment is what brings an entity into the product's scope. Supplementation is what the product reads about it. Writeback is what it writes back to close the loop. Foundational synchronization is the reference data the two systems must agree on. Shared context is how identity and session are shared. [integration-touchpoints.md](../02-research/integration-touchpoints.md) carries the source and the examples on three systems of record.

Typed this way, the fill fraction of an adjacent market is arithmetic: the share of its touchpoints the seller already knows from markets that carry the same types. The seats a workflow typically carries are partly derivable from its touchpoint types too, because the seats that police a touchpoint follow its type. Whoever owns the record polices writeback, whoever owns the operation polices enrollment, whoever owns master data polices synchronization, and security polices shared context. That is the spine doing work for the bargaining ledger and not only the enforcement one. And the product is already this library, because a product is an encoded reference workflow, so a seller keeps the library by annotating the product's own step list with what each market has taught about each step. An empty touchpoint then reads two ways at once: as market ignorance the seller must pay to fill, and as a product gap the seller must build.

**Why one ledger fails.** Search content lives at the market and would be copied into every deal's file and drift there. And the seat ledger records exposure, which is different information from the step ledger's divergence, read by different people at different moments.

**Every ledger is shared, and this is derived rather than chosen.** The gap a ledger tracks is a party's uncertainty about their own outcome, and only that party can close it. A search ledger the buyer cannot read alone has not travelled. A seat ledger whose occupant has not read their own row has confirmed nothing, and the Test cell has not happened. An enforcement ledger whose buyer column the seller filled alone is a map of the seller's guesses. So each ledger is addressed to the party whose uncertainty it tracks, and a ledger withheld from that party is not an instrument. Tact belongs in how a row is written, above all the casualty's, and the seller's private notes are a separate document that closes no gap.

**How the enforcement ledger grows.** It is the clearest case, and the other two follow its pattern.

| Stage | Column appended | Filled by | What the buyer sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before the deal | Reference step, role, system, data | Seller, once | The end state: every row will end as "matches" or "gated" |
| Map | The buyer's version of the step. Matches, diverges, or missing. Exception paths with volumes. Systems with schemas. Seats that touch the step. | Both, in discovery | Their own workflow beside the reference, and every divergence named as a risk |
| Test | For each divergence, the scenario run against it in their environment, the result, and the skeptic's verdict. Resolved, mitigated, or product limit acknowledged in writing. | Seller runs, buyer's skeptic judges | Proof against their risks rather than a demonstration of the product |
| Stage | For each risk not resolved, the action that retires it, its owner on either side, its date, and the contract stage it gates. | Both, signed | Why each gate exists, and what they owe to pass it |
| Maintain | Which risks materialized, which new divergences appeared, and re-mapping at each review. | Both, at each review | Where the moat has grown and where the gap is reopening |

The buyer sees the end state from the first meeting because the empty columns are visible. The empty cells are the seller's ignorance shown rather than hidden, and how many columns are filled before signature is how much of the cost was paid before signature, which is Axiom II's second clause made countable. The first gate in the Stage column is the minimum viable integration: the smallest set of touchpoints that lets the buyer verify value goes live first, and the later-phase types arrive as later gates.

The seat ledger grows the same way with a seat per row: the seat and the steps it touches, then the occupant and what they are measured on, then the stated exposure with its sign, then the containment or the decision criteria the occupant has signed, then the re-statement when the occupant changes. The search ledger grows before the deal and is maintained at the market: the alternative, the steps it covers, the evidence that compares it, the path by which a buyer reaches it, and the entrants appended as the category drifts.

**Divergence after signature.** A buyer who moves away from the reference workflow after go-live raises their own specific investment, and with it the enforcement cost any challenger would face. This is the Maintain cell of the enforcement column, and [04-seller-surplus-model.md](./04-seller-surplus-model.md) section 7 sets its two conditions. The divergence must be the buyer's and disclosed, because an anticipated lock-in is priced at signature. And it is healthy only while the seller's own bond grows as fast as its temptation to exploit the position. Under those conditions it is a moat. Without them it is the extraction drift Axiom II names.

## 6. The plan

The plan is not a fourth document. It is the empty cells across the three ledgers, ordered and bounded.

- **Ordered by direction.** Axiom I. The cells on the component that binds after amplification come first. A deal that reads bargaining-dominant works the seat ledger's empty cells before the step ledger's, however much the seller would rather run a demonstration.
- **Bounded by level.** Axiom II. The level says how much apparatus the deal can carry, so it caps how many cells the seller fills before the cost of filling them exceeds what they save. Below the boundary the plan is empty and the buyer verifies by trying.
- **Front-loaded by specificity.** Axiom II's second clause. The share of cells filled before signature rises with the level.
- **Checked on both sides.** The plan must leave the buyer's surplus positive at the moment of decision and the seller's surplus positive across the repetitions the frequency allows. A plan that closes the deal and cannot be recovered is the Structural one-shot case, and the answer is to restructure or decline rather than to run the plan lighter.
- **Re-derived at every boundary.** Closing a gap rotates the direction, so the order of the remaining cells changes after each artifact lands. A plan whose order never changes is a plan in which nothing was learned.

The plan therefore has a cost the seller can read before committing to it. The cells it orders are the seller's spend, and at the market level the same reading is the cost to serve.

## 7. What this does not settle

- **The five verbs are read off one instrument set.** They generalize the shape of the four-artifact implementation chain the repository carried before this derivation, and they are stated here as the hypothesis the bargaining and search sets will test. Enforcement is the one bilateral pair, which is why it needs both a Map and a Test, and the one-sided pairs may run on fewer beats.
- **Search, Stage bundles two activities.** A channel that carries a stake and a trial that hands verification to the buyer may be two verbs, and the cell may split.
- **Bargaining, Stage may describe the incumbent qualification practice** rather than anything the framework adds. The test is whether the framework's version derives from the seat definition, which the incumbent practice lacks.
- **The search ledger's row.** An alternative is the natural unit for the buyer. A step is the natural unit for the spine. Which one the ledger is keyed on is open.
- **Whether Test is a distinct row for bargaining**, per section 4.
- **Section 2.3 is a conjecture** with a stated falsifier and no test.
- **The five touchpoint types are asserted general.** They are shown on three systems of record and tested on none.
- **Equilibrium is not treated here.** [07-open-questions.md](./07-open-questions.md) item 25 records that the components have drift rates and no equilibrium statement, and the drift section of [01-motions.md](./01-motions.md) is where it belongs.
- **The instruments themselves.** This file derives what each must do. None yet exists in the form derived here.
