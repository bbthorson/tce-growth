# From Axioms to Instruments

**Version:** 1.3
**Purpose:** To derive, from the three axioms and the two definitions, what a reader must be able to do and in what order: determine the state of the three transaction costs, plan their minimization, and execute the plan with the buyer. This file names activities and outcomes. It names no tool. The instruments are built from it and trace to it, and nothing here is inherited from the practice it will replace.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Everything in this file is a consequence.** Each section states which axiom or definition it follows from. A row in the activity grid, a column in a ledger or a rule about who reads what that cannot be traced back is a mistake, and section 7 records what is asserted rather than derived.

---

## 1. Three phases, two levels

The [Constitution](./00-tcg-constitution.md) states three decisions in time order: whether the buyer uses the market at all, what the deal costs each party today, and what it might cost them later. Read in order, they give the three things a reader has to be able to do.

| Phase | What the reader must be able to do | From | Unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Determine the state** | Read each cost's position against its own thresholds, where the sale starts, and whether anything specific is sunk, first for a market and then for a deal | Axiom I for the positions, Axiom III for the specific exposure | Market, then deal |
| **Plan the minimization** | Choose which costs to pay down and which gaps to close, in what order, with what evidence, at what investment by each party, and how much of the allocation to settle before anything specific is sunk | Axioms II and III together | Deal, bounded by the market reading |
| **Execute with the buyer** | Run the activities the plan orders and re-read where the sale starts at every boundary | Axiom I orders, Axiom II pays, Axiom III stages and closes | Deal |

**Diagnosis has two levels because Axiom I's decision is read twice.** Across a buyer population, where the sale starts is the motion, and choosing which costs to be built to pay down is a decision about what an organization builds and staffs. That decision is taken before any deal is met, and Axiom II's pursuit corollary decides whether to take it at all. For one buyer, the same positions, together with Axiom III's reading of what the deal sinks, flex the apparatus around the motion the market reading chose. So the market reading comes first and the deal reading varies around it. A book of deal readings that disagrees with its market reading is the check on the market reading, and the disagreement is worth more than either number.

**The plan is the phase the framework has never had.** Every earlier instrument read a deal and then jumped to an instrument set. Nothing between them said what the seller was trying to make true, or what it would cost them to do it. Section 6 supplies it, and it turns out to be a reading of the other instruments rather than an instrument of its own.

## 2. Determining the state

### 2.1 The market reading

A market is the buyers who call a problem by one name, and Axiom I is read across that population coarsely on purpose. What can be counted about a population before any buyer is met, by cost:

| Cost | What is countable about the population | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| **Search and information** (the field's *search*) | Whether buyers can name the problem, how many alternatives they enumerate, whether published material lets them compare, and whether this seller has a path to them | How much of the buyer's search cannot be resolved without a seller present |
| **Bargaining and decision** (the field's *consensus*) | The decision roles the reference workflow carries at the typical buyer, and whether a formal body sits over them | How large the coalition is before anyone is met |
| **Policing and enforcement** (the field's *implementation*) | Who codified the workflow, a regulator or each buyer, how deep the integration surface runs, and how far the typical buyer diverges from the reference | How much adaptation a deal needs, how specific it is, and whether the population converges or fragments |

The reading emits each cost's position for the population, where the sale starts, and whether the typical deal sinks anything specific. Where the sale starts is the motion the organization must build, and a population where every cost is self-serve and nothing specific is sunk is Turnkey. It then emits a second thing the buyer's positions cannot: **the seller's cost to serve, by cost.** By Axiom II's cost-to-serve corollary, the seller's investment falls on whichever cost it pays down, so the same buyer positions cost two differently built sellers different amounts. Axiom II's pursuit corollary then closes the reading: can this population's contract value and frequency recover the investment its costs demand from this seller. Pursue, restructure, or decline.

The organization-level failure this reading prevents is the one the Constitution names as imitation: a motion adopted because it worked for a seller in a different market, aimed at a cost this market's buyers do not carry.

**The reading accumulates, and its fill is the seller's differentiation.** What can be counted about a population is not fixed. It is a ledger the seller fills from every deal reading that feeds it, and how much of it is filled is how much unique insight the seller holds about that market. [04-seller-surplus-model.md](./04-seller-surplus-model.md) section 7.5 carries the seller's side. Three consequences follow for the reading itself. It emits a further quantity, the unfilled fraction, which is the seller's own market-level ignorance and the honest measure of how far to trust the rest. The cost to serve rises with the unfilled fraction, because every Map cell is being filled for the first time. And an entrant whose ledger is thin cannot verify cheaply, the third of Axiom III's three levers, so they are pushed onto the two that only move surplus between the parties, price and risk, until the ledger fills. That is why an entrant prices below an entrenched competitor, and because verification is the only lever that raises the joint surplus, it is why the entrant's margin is thin. The floor is the entry rule: a seller with an empty ledger has no reading to act on and should not be in the market.

The unfilled fraction is a seller cost on all three columns. Among the gaps, it lands most directly on the policing pair, where the seller's ignorance is one half and drives the seller's own chance of future loss under Axiom III.

### 2.2 The deal reading

A deal is one buyer's divergence from the reference workflow, with the decision roles their version carries. The deal reading emits the quantities the Constitution names: each cost's position and where the sale starts, which move whenever discovery changes what is counted, the specific exposure, which decides the future cost and the governance form under Axiom III, the frequency, the three gaps, which feed each party's chance of future loss, and the mandate: whether the initiative still holds executive sponsorship and allocated capital, read apart from the decision roles, because a mandate dissolving overrides every role and its loss often arrives disguised as one occupant's departure.

Each gap is its own pair's uncertainty about its own outcome, per Axiom III, and this file states the bargaining gap at the level of the claim so that no instrument can inherit the older proxy.

- **The search gap** is the buyer's, about the market. It is the share of what the buyer needs in order to rank the alternatives that they do not yet hold as evidence.
- **The bargaining gap** is each decision role's, about its own outcome. It is the share of decision roles whose occupant has not stated what the change does to the thing they are measured on, either as a reservation the arrangement can answer or as a confirmation that it costs them nothing. The sign is read from the statement. The statement is recorded as a reservation and its answer, never as a verdict on the person. A decision role the seller has mapped but whose occupant has not confirmed is an open gap, however good the seller's map, and a stated hedge is the gap observed directly.
- **The policing gap** (the field's implementation gap) is bilateral, the seller's about the buyer's environment and the buyer's about the seller's capability, and it is the one pair whose two halves are read separately and summed. Each half drives its own party's chance of future loss.

### 2.3 Definition works on the gaps

*This subsection is a conjecture. It follows from the axioms and nothing has tested it.*

A codified workflow lets the buyer name what they need, lets the seller map it, and lets each decision role state what the change does to it. So definition lowers all three gaps. It does not lower the costs themselves, and it raises one of them along with what the deal sinks. A workflow is codified because people are measured on it, so a change to it touches more decision roles and produces more casualties, and the bargaining cost rises. A codified workflow is an embedded one, so the adaptation either party must invest is larger, and more of it is specific. Only the search cost can fall, and only when codification converges across buyers, which is what a regulator produces and a single buyer does not.

Definition is therefore a verifiability variable and belongs to Axiom III. A mature, codified deal is easier to run and no cheaper to buy. *Falsifier:* a book of deals in which workflow maturity predicts lower cost with size held constant.

## 3. What an instrument is

The three axioms constrain an instrument in three ways, and together they define one.

1. **Axiom I indexes and orders it.** An instrument works on one cost. A motion is the set of instruments for the cost where the sale starts, and an instrument aimed at a cost the buyer already pays down alone leaves the deal where it was.
2. **Axiom II prices it.** Every instrument is an investment by one party against one cost, and each party goes ahead only while its share is covered by its own return. Moving an instrument from one party to the other changes the total only when the party taking it on does the work more cheaply or lowers a future loss by doing it.
3. **Axiom III times it and says what it produces.** Where nothing specific is sunk and a trial verifies fit, the set is empty and the buyer verifies by trying. Otherwise the allocation must be settled before the specific investment is sunk, so an instrument set is sequenced, and what it settles early is who bears which adaptation risk and how the commitment is staged. The work of adaptation itself can follow, and under uncertainty it must. What the instrument produces is evidence that closes a named pair's uncertainty about their own outcome, which lowers that party's chance of future loss. The evidence is a signal, and by the Friction Allocation Principles it counts only if it cannot be automated away, is borne by the party making the claim, scales with the size of the claim, and is adjudicated by someone who loses when it is wrong. Fail one and the instrument is cheap talk.

**Two classes.** Most instruments close a gap. A second class lowers a cost itself, and the framework has always had members of it without naming the class: restructuring a one-shot sale as a recurring one buys mutual sanction and lowers what governing a specific deal costs, reducing incentive variance in the coalition lowers the bargaining cost, and designing the product closer to the workflow a population runs lowers divergence and with it what a buyer must sink. The plan in section 6 covers both classes, because a workshop that gets a committee to state its trade-offs aloud is lowering variance, and calling that a gap-closer misdescribes what it does.

**Five verbs.** An instrument does one of five things to a cost, and each verb is defined the same way whichever cost it acts on.

| Verb | What it does | Traces to |
|---|---|---|
| **Read** | Counts what can be seen from outside a population or a deal | Axiom I's positions, Axiom III's specific exposure |
| **Map** | Learns what is inside one buyer, and closes the gap on the side that can be closed by asking | Axiom III |
| **Test** | Produces the costly signal that closes the gap on the side that can only be closed by proof | Axiom III and the Friction Allocation Principles |
| **Stage** | Reallocates exposure and control so that one irreversible decision becomes a sequence | Axiom III's governance and staged-commitment corollaries |
| **Maintain** | Re-earns what drift erodes, at the rate each gap rebuilds | Axiom III's rebuild clause |

Read and Map differ by vantage, outside against inside. Map and Test close gaps. Stage and Maintain do something else, and a reader who treats the five as five kinds of proof will build the wrong instruments for the last two.

## 4. The activity grid

Five verbs on three costs. Each cell is an activity and the outcome it works toward. No cell names a tool.

| | **Search and information** (buyer against the market) | **Bargaining and decision** (each decision role against its own outcome) | **Policing and enforcement** (seller and buyer against each other) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Read** | Whether this buyer population can name the problem, enumerate its alternatives, compare them on published material, and be reached by this seller at all. Outcome: how much of the buyer's search cannot be resolved without a seller present. | The decision roles the workflow carries, how many can say no, whether a formal body sits over them, and how many occupants have stated their own exposure. Outcome: the size of the coalition and the share of it whose outcome is known to itself. | How far the buyer's workflow diverges from the reference, how many systems exchange data, how many procedures change, how many exception paths are undocumented, and how much of that either side has evidence for. Outcome: the specificity of the investment and both halves of the gap. |
| **Map** | Define what the buyer is choosing between: the problem's boundary, the alternative set including build and do nothing, and the reference workflow the product speaks to. Outcome: the buyer can name the problem and locate the product inside the set. | For each decision role, what the change does to the thing its occupant is measured on: budget, headcount, standing, control. Recorded as the occupant's reservation and what the arrangement will do about it. Outcome: every decision role named with its exposure, and the seller knows whose arrangement has to change. | The buyer's environment and workflow as it is: exception paths with volumes, integration points with schemas, the political map of who loses control. Outcome: seller ignorance closed, and the divergence from the reference known step by step. Functionality and data misfits can be mapped here. Misfits in roles, controls and culture surface only in use, so their rows stay empty when the investment is sunk, by construction, and fill under Maintain. |
| **Test** | Verifiable proof that travels without the seller: third-party comparison, reference architectures, produced outcomes a buyer can check alone. Outcome: the buyer ranks alternatives on evidence rather than claims. | Each occupant states their reservation one to one and confirms it is understood. Written positions are collected apart before any room convenes, because a room reinforces what everyone already knows and drops what one member knows alone. Outcome: stated positions replaced by confirmed reservations, and variance visible rather than suppressed. | The seller proves capability in the buyer's environment at the seller's cost, and the buyer's skeptic attacks the plan before signature. Outcome: buyer uncertainty closed, product limits acknowledged in writing, failure modes named with owners. |
| **Stage** | A path to the buyer that carries a stake: a channel, partner or consortium that loses something when a bad match passes. Where nothing specific is sunk, a trial that hands fit verification to the buyer. Outcome: the buyer can reach the seller, and whoever adjudicates between them bears consequences. | Written decision criteria and a decision sequence the coalition agrees to before the vendor decision, with each reservation's answer written in. Outcome: the coalition's decision is a sequence with owners rather than a late veto. | Mutual commitments sequenced into gates, each with a priced right to stop, and residual control allocated for the states nobody specified. Outcome: one irreversible decision becomes a series, and exposure is staged for whoever sinks the specific investment. |
| **Maintain** | Keep the category from re-pooling: refresh the verifiable material as entrants arrive and the name drifts, and watch whether cheap signals are swamping costly ones. Outcome: the population can still rank, and the seller's rank is re-earned. | Re-read the decision roles at every occupant change, reorganization and budget cycle. Outcome: a new occupant states their exposure before the gap reopens into a stall, and a champion's departure is a decision-role event rather than a surprise. | Re-map the environment as staff, systems and workflow change, and re-earn credibility with delivered outcomes. Outcome: the gap stays closed, and the next renewal compares a mapped incumbent against an unmapped challenger. |

**Three observations from filling the grid.**

- **Every cell below Read is a seller investment under Axiom II.** The cells a market's positions call for are that market's cost to serve, and the cells a deal's positions call for are that deal's.
- **Test collapses toward Map on the bargaining column.** An occupant stating their own exposure is the map and the confirmation in one act, and what remains distinct is the collective version, the room in which the objections are heard together. The row is kept because the room is a distinct activity, and section 7 records this as the weakest split in the grid.
- **Stage is where the plan meets governance.** On every column it is the cell that converts a decision into a sequence with owners, which is why it cannot be run before Map and Test have said what the sequence must protect.

## 5. Three ledgers on one spine

The grid says what to do. Axiom III says who must be in the room when it is done, because each cost runs between its own pair of parties. That gives three documents rather than one, each keyed to the unit its pair is about, and each growing to the right as the deal proceeds.

| | **Search and information** | **Bargaining and decision** | **Policing and enforcement** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pair** | Buyer against the market | Each decision role against its own outcome | Seller and buyer against each other |
| **Row** | An alternative the buyer must rule out, including build and do nothing | A decision role | A workflow step |
| **Lives at** | Market level. One per market, written before any deal, reused by every deal in it | Deal level. One per deal | Deal level. One per deal |
| **Filled by** | The seller publishes it. The buyer can complete it alone, which is the point | The seller with each occupant, one at a time | Both parties, in the open |
| **Done when** | The buyer has placed the product in the set on evidence and can reach the seller | Every decision role's reservation is written and answered in the arrangement | Every row matches the reference or carries a gated mitigation with an owner |
| **Shared with the buyer** | Public. It has to travel without the seller | Split. The decision-role map is private to the seller. The arrangement record is shared | Fully, with both parties |

**The spine.** The reference workflow, written once by the seller, is the row key of the enforcement ledger and the index of the other two. Each alternative in the search ledger is compared on which steps it covers. Each decision role in the bargaining ledger is defined by which steps it runs, owns, pays for or polices. The three cross-reference by step without merging, and a change to the reference propagates to all three. A seller who has not written the reference workflow down cannot open any of the three. That is the Constitution's definition of workflow applied before selling begins: a product that cannot name its workflow has no reference to read divergence against.

**How the spine is keyed.** Two workflows share a step only if the steps are typed the same way, so the library the reference workflows are drawn from needs a typing. A reference workflow has two kinds of step: internal steps the product performs alone, and touchpoints where it meets the buyer's system of record. Touchpoints come in five types, and the types hold in any vertical. Enrollment is what brings an entity into the product's scope. Supplementation is what the product reads about it. Writeback is what it writes back to close the loop. Foundational synchronization is the reference data the two systems must agree on. Shared context is how identity and session are shared. [integration-touchpoints.md](../02-research/integration-touchpoints.md) carries the source and the examples on three systems of record.

Typed this way, the fill fraction of an adjacent market is arithmetic: the share of its touchpoints the seller already knows from markets that carry the same types. The decision roles a workflow typically carries are partly derivable from its touchpoint types too, because the roles that police a touchpoint follow its type. Whoever owns the record polices writeback, whoever owns the operation polices enrollment, whoever owns master data polices synchronization, and security polices shared context. That is the spine doing work for the bargaining ledger and not only the enforcement one. And the product is already this library, because a product is an encoded reference workflow, so a seller keeps the library by annotating the product's own step list with what each market has taught about each step. An empty touchpoint then reads two ways at once: as market ignorance the seller must pay to fill, and as a product gap the seller must build.

**Why one ledger fails.** Search content lives at the market and would be copied into every deal's file and drift there. And the decision-role ledger records exposure, which is different information from the step ledger's divergence, read by different people at different moments.

**Who reads each ledger follows from whose gap it closes, and the answer differs by ledger.** The search ledger travels without the seller or it has failed. The enforcement ledger is open to both sides, because a workflow divergence is nobody's career risk, and a buyer column the seller filled alone is a map of the seller's guesses. The bargaining ledger splits, and an external review is why. An occupant closes their own gap by receiving the answer, the changed arrangement, and not by reading the record of their concern. A manager survives on calculated ambiguity and will not write that a change costs them headcount on a document their peers can find, however the row is framed. So there are two documents. A private decision-role map, seller-internal, filled one to one, from which the gap metric is counted and which is never shared. And a shared arrangement record, visible to the buyer, carrying what the commercial terms now do and for whom, with no attribution to anyone's exposure. Reservations are documented in order to restructure the arrangement, and the restructuring is the thing shared. Tact belongs in the private map. The arrangement record needs none, because it names terms and not people. [tce-empirical-record.md](../02-research/tce-empirical-record.md) carries the critique this answers.

**How the enforcement ledger grows.** It is the clearest case, and the other two follow its pattern.

| Stage | Column appended | Filled by | What the buyer sees |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before the deal | Reference step, role, system, data | Seller, once | The end state: every row will end as "matches" or "gated" |
| Map | The buyer's version of the step. Matches, diverges, or missing. Exception paths with volumes. Systems with schemas. Decision roles that touch the step. | Both, in discovery | Their own workflow beside the reference, and every divergence named as a risk |
| Test | For each divergence, the scenario run against it in their environment, the result, and the skeptic's verdict. Resolved, mitigated, or product limit acknowledged in writing. | Seller runs, buyer's skeptic judges | Proof against their risks rather than a demonstration of the product |
| Stage | For each risk not resolved, the action that retires it, its owner on either side, its date, and the contract stage it gates. | Both, signed | Why each gate exists, and what they owe to pass it |
| Maintain | Which risks materialized, which new divergences appeared, and re-mapping at each review. | Both, at each review | Where the moat has grown and where the gap is reopening |

The buyer sees the end state from the first meeting because the empty columns are visible. The empty cells are the seller's ignorance shown rather than hidden. How many Stage cells carry an owner and a gate before the first specific investment is sunk is how much of the arrangement was settled in advance, which is Axiom III's allocation clause made countable. The Map and Test columns can be honestly incomplete at that point for the misfits only use reveals. The first gate in the Stage column is the minimum viable integration: the smallest set of touchpoints that lets the buyer verify value goes live first, and the later-phase types arrive as later gates.

The decision-role map grows the same way with a decision role per row: the role and the steps it touches, then the occupant and what they are measured on, then the reservation as the occupant states it one to one, then whether the mandate behind the role still stands, then the re-statement when the occupant changes. The arrangement record grows beside it with a term per row: what changed in the commercial terms, which decision role it answers without naming the reservation, and who on each side owns it. The search ledger grows before the deal and is maintained at the market: the alternative, the steps it covers, the evidence that compares it, the path by which a buyer reaches it, and the entrants appended as the category drifts.

**Divergence after signature.** A buyer who moves away from the reference workflow after go-live raises their own specific investment, and with it the adaptation any challenger would have to pay for. This is the Maintain cell of the enforcement column, and [04-seller-surplus-model.md](./04-seller-surplus-model.md) section 7 sets its two conditions. The divergence must be the buyer's and disclosed, because an anticipated lock-in is priced at signature. And it is healthy only while the seller's own bond grows as fast as its temptation to exploit the position. Under those conditions it is a moat. Without them it is the extraction drift Axiom III names.

## 6. The plan

The plan is not a fourth document. It is the empty cells across the three ledgers, ordered and bounded.

- **Ordered by where the sale starts.** Axiom I. The cells on the cost nearest its own participation threshold come first, and two costs at the same position are run together. A deal whose sale starts at bargaining works the decision-role ledger's empty cells before the step ledger's, however much the seller would rather run a demonstration. A cost above its participation threshold comes before everything, because no work on the other two reaches it.
- **Bounded by each party's return.** Axiom II. Every cell filled is an investment by one party, so the seller fills cells only while its share stays inside what the relationship can return, and moves a cell to the buyer only where the buyer does it more cheaply. Where nothing specific is sunk and a trial verifies fit, the plan is empty and the buyer verifies by trying.
- **Allocated before the investment is sunk.** Axiom III. What rises with specificity is the share of the arrangement settled before anyone sinks the specific investment: who bears which adaptation risk, staged how, with what right to stop. What is spent in advance can fall as uncertainty rises, because discovery cannot reach what only use reveals. The minimum viable integration goes live first and the rest is staged, and a plan that tries to resolve every cell in advance produces Axiom III's over-frictioned failure and the buyer fatigue the indecision research in [fear-of-failure.md](../02-research/fear-of-failure.md) describes.
- **Checked on both sides.** The plan must leave the buyer's condition positive at the moment of decision and the seller's condition positive across the repetitions the frequency allows. A plan that closes the deal and cannot be recovered is the specific one-shot case, Axiom II's unamortizable failure, and the answer is to restructure as recurring or decline rather than to run the plan lighter.
- **Re-derived at every boundary.** Discovery changes what is counted, so where the sale starts can move after each artifact lands, and each gap closed changes which future loss is left to stage. A plan whose order never changes is a plan in which nothing was learned.

The plan therefore has a cost the seller can read before committing to it. The cells it orders are the seller's investment, and at the market level the same reading is the cost to serve.

## 7. What this does not settle

- **The five verbs are read off one instrument set.** They generalize the shape of the four-artifact implementation chain the repository carried before this derivation, and they are stated here as the hypothesis the bargaining and search sets will test. Policing and enforcement is the one bilateral pair, which is why it needs both a Map and a Test, and the one-sided pairs may run on fewer beats.
- **Search, Stage bundles two activities.** A channel that carries a stake and a trial that hands verification to the buyer may be two verbs, and the cell may split.
- **Bargaining, Stage may describe the incumbent qualification practice** rather than anything the framework adds. The test is whether the framework's version derives from the decision-role definition, which the incumbent practice lacks.
- **The search ledger's row.** An alternative is the natural unit for the buyer. A step is the natural unit for the spine. Which one the ledger is keyed on is open.
- **Whether Test is a distinct row for bargaining**, per section 4.
- **Section 2.3 is a conjecture** with a stated falsifier and no test.
- **The five touchpoint types are asserted general.** They are shown on three systems of record and tested on none.
- **Equilibrium is not treated here.** [07-open-questions.md](./07-open-questions.md) item 27 records that the gaps have drift rates and no equilibrium statement.
- **Whether occupants will state reservations one to one at the rate the metric needs.** An external review established that they will not write personal exposure on a shared instrument, which is why the bargaining ledger split. Whether they will say it privately often enough for the gap to be counted, and whether the shared arrangement record gets ratified, is the decision-role map's first field test.
- **The instruments themselves.** This file derives what each must do. [The Market Reading](../../practice/00-market-reading.md) is the first built from it. The deal reading, the plan and the three ledgers do not yet exist in the form derived here.
