# Open Questions

**Version:** 1.3
**Purpose:** To record, in one place, where the theory is under-developed now that the axioms are fixed. Each entry says what the framework claims, what it lacks, and what would settle it. Entries are grouped by the axiom they weaken. The register holds what writing or an instrument can settle. A claim that only evidence can settle sits in [06-calibration.md](./06-calibration.md) section 4.1 under the number it was given here.

This register consolidates. Where a file already carries its own "what this does not settle" section, the entry here points at it rather than restating it. An entry leaves this file when the work is done or when the claim is withdrawn, and either exit is recorded in the closed list at the end. Item numbers are stable, because other files cite them, so a closed number is not reused. Closed and moved numbers are listed at the end.

## Axiom I, composition

**5. Reachability has research and no measurement.** The search cost's second blocker, a buyer the seller cannot reach, is backed by [channel-collapse.md](../02-research/channel-collapse.md) and read in the [Deal Triage Calculator](../../practice/deal-triage-calculator.md) as a yes or no: a missing channel puts search in the keeps-out zone whatever the count. *Settled by:* a count-based reading of reachability that survives the same scrutiny as the other counts.

**6. The market reading has no worked case.** The motion is chosen across a buyer population, and Axiom I's seller limit decides whether to enter it at all, which are organizational decisions. Constitution 6.0 made the seller's limit half of whether a market exists, so the reading now carries a claim of the axiom's own rather than of a corollary. Without a reading of the population, the choice of motion is made by imitation, the organization-level failure the Constitution names. [08-from-axioms-to-instruments.md](./08-from-axioms-to-instruments.md) section 2.1 derives what the reading must contain, and [The Market Reading](../../practice/00-market-reading.md) 3.0 is built from it for Constitution 6.1. *Settled by:* two real populations, chosen and read by someone other than the reading's author, with where the sale starts predicted before the first deals and compared with where the Deal Triage Calculator reads it on them. Then a book of deal readings checked against the market reading they sit under.

**49. Policing and enforcement holds three different things.** Dahlman's policing is monitoring after the contract. The framework also files under it the buyer's uncertainty about fit before the contract, which is information and overlaps search's "does this one fit?", and the labor of integration, which Williamson counts as production cost. One threshold then gates all three, and the cost straddles the line between Axioms II and III. Raised by the 6.1 adversarial review. *Settled by:* a statement of which part of the policing cost is a transaction cost, where the other parts go, and why they share one participation threshold or do not.

**50. Non-compensation may follow from the bargaining cost.** By the Coase theorem, payers who could move budget inside the buyer at no cost would let ease on one cost make up for another. So the costs stay apart only because moving budget between payers is itself costly, which is the bargaining cost, and $F_k$ does two jobs: an amount that adds in the conditions and a capacity that gates participation. Raised by the 6.1 adversarial review. *Settled by:* a split of each $F_k$ into the part the seller can absorb or the buyer's payers can reallocate, which adds, and the part neither can, which gates, with non-compensation derived from the bargaining cost.

**54. A seller may enter a market to learn whether it exists.** Axiom I's seller limit says a seller enters only where the deals it wins repay what it builds first. Sellers also enter as a bet, pricing design partners at a loss to find out whether the market is there, with no outside capital, which item 44 covers. Raised by the 6.1 convergence check. *Settled by:* a statement of whether what a seller learns counts as a return under its limit, and how the market reading would show it.

**56. The missing-capability count does not measure what the seller limit needs.** The seller's limit rests on a fixed build no single deal repays, net of what the seller could redeploy. The Market Reading counts missing activities 0 to 4, which cannot tell a fixed build from spending repeated in every deal, weighs a template the same as a field team, and ignores whether a capability carries over from an adjacent market. Building or partnering is then Williamson's make-or-buy, which turns on that specificity. Raised by the practice-rebuild review. *Settled by:* a reading that separates fixed from per-deal capabilities and scores how specific each is to this market.

**58. The bargaining cost's bands saturate, and nothing tests them.** $F_{consensus}$ at $\alpha = 1$ is not a fraction of annual contract value, so its bands sit on a scale of their own, and a coalition of nine or more reads High at the lowest variance, so the bands do not separate enterprise deals. They can also call for a safeguard while the triage's count-based position reads within reach, and nothing says which governs. Raised by the practice-rebuild review. *Settled by:* bands rescaled to the count the triage reads or to contract value, and a book of deals logging the gap, the variance and the band at open against each outcome: close, competitive loss, no decision, drift after signature. The bands fail if no-decision losses do not concentrate in High, or if the gap at signature does not predict drift after it.

**59. A sponsor staked on success is a party to the dispute, not its adjudicator.** The Bargaining Ledger's safeguard leans on a staked sponsor, and the overrule of an unanswerable role runs through whoever holds authority over it. A sponsor whose measure rides on the initiative gains from overruling reservations and from passing bad news upward (Staw 1976), so it fails the fourth Friction Allocation Principle as an adjudicator. Raised by the practice-rebuild review. *Settled by:* a statement of what neutral adjudication between roles inside the buyer requires, and who besides the sponsor can hold it.

**57. The policing gap routes on fractions that a handful of rows can flip.** The Enforcement Ledger's two halves are shares of rows in scope, so on a deal with few rows one verdict moves a half by a third or more, across the 0.25 routing margin and the 0.625 hold. The band edges were carried by arithmetic from the scorecard's 4.0 and 7.0, and never re-derived for the ledger's rows. Raised by the practice-rebuild review. *Settled by:* a stated row count below which each half is reported as counts and does not route, and band edges read from a book of ledgers.

## Axiom II, investment

Constitution 4.0 made investment an axiom of its own. Items 7 to 13 were filed here when Axiom II carried specificity, and they now sit under Axiom III with their numbers unchanged. Item 34, on reading a cost floor, sits under cross-cutting, and 6.0 moved the cost floor to Axiom I. Items 38 and 39 wait on data and sit in the calibration list.

**45. Discovery spending should predict retention, and nothing reads it.** Constitution 6.0 says seller spending adds value only when the seller does the work more cheaply or the work lowers the cost at time 1, and that without retention sales spending is a price cut. Its falsifier needs cost of sale split by what it bought, discovery and verification against persuasion, and no instrument records the split. *Settled by:* a cost-of-sale ledger that tags each activity as verification the buyer can check or as persuasion, by the [Friction Allocation Diagnostic](../../practice/friction-allocation-diagnostic.md)'s principles, and a book of deals where the split can be read against net revenue retention. Constitution 6.1 splits the claim into two jobs, raising what the deal creates and letting a deal that creates value go ahead, so the ledger tags each activity by the job it did and not by the artifact, since one security packet can do both. It also needs a tag for price capture, which tests how much of what representatives do is absorption at all. The test has to compare spending within each representative and segment, code it without sight of the outcome, and keep the deals discovery disqualified in the count, since discovery raises retention by filtering alone.

**48. A rival can raise this buyer's recognized cost of a seller.** In a competitive deal a rival seeds the request for proposal with requirements the seller fails, raises doubts about its integration, or forces a bake-off. That is raising rivals' costs (Salop and Scheffman 1983), aimed at $\tilde{r}_k$, and the Constitution moves $\tilde{r}_k$ only through the category prior and the seller's own evidence. Raised by the 6.1 adversarial review. *Settled by:* a statement of whether a competitive deal is read per vendor, and of how a seller defends its recognized cost against a rival's.

**44. Outside capital can hold a market's costs below what its floor supports.** Venture funding lets sellers invest against a market's costs, search above all, at a loss no deal repays, and so open a market whose floor sits above the participation threshold, or make one look Turnkey that is not. [01-motions.md](./01-motions.md) section 6 says who pays to open a market and is silent on investment that is never repaid. *Settled by:* a statement of what the market reading shows when the seller's condition is funded from outside it, and of whether the market stays open when the funding ends.

## Axiom III, future cost

**12. The unified-governance boundary has a reading and no value.** The fourth governance row says the buyer eventually builds it. [05-governance-forms.md](./05-governance-forms.md) section 3 says when: once the buyer fails the bond condition, it builds when what the seller can take at a boundary exceeds the buyer's build margin, its own running cost plus the annualized build and migration plus any value its version would lack, less the price. The [Sustaining Adoption Review](../../practice/adoption-review.md) now records the buyer's alternatives at renewal, a challenger evaluated, its in-house team on the adjacent function and its own build estimate, and prices a buyer past the bond condition within them. Nothing values the margin from them. *Settled by:* the build margin read in contract value from those alternatives, and a book of renewals checking whether buyers that fail the bond condition build where the margin says they will.

**51. A failure after signature can always be explained after the fact.** Inside Axiom III a verified deal that fails has three explanations: the evidence went stale, the fit moved, or the other side held it up in plain view. Read afterward, any failure fits one of them, and verification that finds misfit stops deals before signature, so the signed deals it is compared on are already screened. Raised by the 6.1 adversarial review. The [Sustaining Adoption Review](../../practice/adoption-review.md) now codes each finding by cause the day the standing review finds it, from the change logs and signed by both sides, and records an exit with no earlier finding as uncoded. That gives the first half a record, though the account manager who keeps it is paid on the renewal. *Settled by:* a book of those records read against churn and contraction, and a count of the deals verification stopped before signature.

## Cross-cutting

**47. Expansion is a time-0 deal inside a time-1 relationship, and the theory does not read it.** Axiom III asks what prevents churn and contraction, so time 1 is all downside, yet much of net revenue retention above 100 percent comes from expansion. An expansion has its own three costs: search near zero, policing partly prepaid by what the buyer has already sunk, and bargaining often reopened by a new budget owner. Raised by the 6.1 adversarial review. *Settled by:* a reading of an expansion's three costs, and a statement of whether the buyer's sunk investment is the seller's leverage there or its exposure.

**34. A cost floor and the category prior have readings and no test.** Axiom I's cost floor corollary says part of each cost is set outside the deal, and the deal reading reports only each cost's position. Constitution 5.2 adds the category prior, what buyers believe each cost is before they meet any seller, and a floor above the participation threshold and a prior above it show the same sign. Telling them apart needs both read. The [Market Reading](../../practice/00-market-reading.md) 3.0 now reads both: a prior column on every ledger row, sourced from what buyers ask before discovery, the length of their questionnaires and requirement lists, and what analysts and peers say the category costs, and a floor-or-prior check for any cost past its edge, asking whether the count would change with a different seller. *Settled by:* a market-level reading of each cost floor, such as vendor count against buyer attention for search, the buyer's approval rules for bargaining and the attributability of outcomes for policing, taken apart from the deal and compared across deals in the same market. The review proposes a natural experiment in the home case. The 21st Century Cures Act required certified EHRs to offer a standardized API, and ONC's public Certified Health IT Product List (CHPL) records when each product was certified under 170.315(g)(10). That is a drop in the policing floor shared across deals. If the corollary holds, hospitals' adoption of third-party apps should jump once their EHR's API went live, by more than any single seller's spending moved it before. Nobody has checked that hospital-level adoption data with the needed dates is public.

## Closed since 4.0

- **8. The level conflated specificity with size.** The summed level retired, and specific exposure is now read on its own count, in Step 3 of the [Deal Triage Calculator](../../practice/deal-triage-calculator.md).
- **16. The coefficient $a$ crossed three boundaries by analogy.** It retired with the reduced form it sat in, and the case for verification over discounting now follows from price cancelling between the two conditions.
- **25. The equation stopped at signature.** The two conditions carry each party's expected future loss, its rebuild and its staging over gates, and recurrence enters through price and delivery cost summed over renewals. What remains of the time index is item 7.
- **26. The notation carried the field names.** 4.0 decided to keep the field subscripts, and the Constitution states why: the field names say what a seller experiences and Dahlman's say what the cost is.
- **13. The research file presented the decomposition as inherited.** [transaction-cost-economics.md](../02-research/transaction-cost-economics.md) now credits Coase with the question, Dahlman with the three costs and Williamson with the split by time, and names what the framework adds.
- **30. Whether the participation threshold rises with the value at stake.** Settled after 4.0, on 2026-10-09: it does not. The threshold marks the buyer's capacity to get through a cost, and value lives in the buyer's condition, where a bigger prize funds the investment that pulls a cost back under the threshold. [02-mathematical-models.md](./02-mathematical-models.md) section 6 carries the argument and its falsifier.
- **20. Value exogeneity is strong.** Settled by Constitution 5.0 on 2026-11-02. Value splits into realizable value, set by the offer and the buyer's situation, recognized value, the estimate the seller moves, and realized value, what the buyer captures. The boundary the entry asked for is between moving recognized value with evidence the buyer can check, which lowers the search cost, and moving it without narrowing the buyer's range, which is persuasion carried as Axiom III exposure. The objection that survives is item 32.
- **31. The Friction Efficiency Index rewarded work done before signature.** Settled on 2026-10-09: its target is now the allocation coverage ratio, the share of specific investment sunk only after an allocation covering it was agreed. The index was archived on 2026-11-09. The coverage ratio lives in the [Enforcement Ledger](../../practice/enforcement-ledger.md), and the comparison with the effort ratio is [06-calibration.md](./06-calibration.md) section 4 item 6.
- **9. Joint exposure had a form and no bond condition.** Settled on 2026-11-04. [02-mathematical-models.md](./02-mathematical-models.md) section 5.5 states it in the two quasi-rents: mutual exposure is a bond for a party while its share of the joint quasi-rent is covered by the other side's quasi-rent and what forcing a renegotiation would cost the other side. Hold-up enters $\pi_p$ as a second factor, and the imbalance, not the level, creates the hazard.
- **11. Frequency was three readings by convention.** Settled on 2026-11-04. [05-governance-forms.md](./05-governance-forms.md) section 1 reads them as regions of each party's stake at the next boundary, $F_p$ from the bond condition. One-shot is no stake on one side or both. Recurrent is a stake on both sides with the buyer still bonded. Continuous is the buyer failing the bond condition because its own quasi-rent has outgrown the seller's stake. The stake is named and not valued.
- **7. The allocation clause had no formula.** Settled in theory on 2026-11-04. [02-mathematical-models.md](./02-mathematical-models.md) section 5.6 derives the allocation required from the bond condition, which showed it rises with the imbalance between the two sunk investments rather than with specificity as such, and Constitution 5.1 restates the mechanism. The work done in advance has a placeholder form that falls with the share of misfit only use reveals. The randomized test moved to the calibration list under the same number.
- **10. Governance form and where the sale starts might interact.** Settled on 2026-11-04. [05-governance-forms.md](./05-governance-forms.md) section 2 says what each form safeguards and checks it against each cost's pair of parties. The forms govern the buyer and the seller, which is the policing pair. A deal whose sale starts at bargaining also needs a safeguard inside the buyer, a staked sponsor and an arrangement each exposed decision role ratifies, because the party that defects there is a role. Search leaves the form unchanged. The falsifier is in that section, and the instrument that reads the internal safeguard belongs to item 2.
- **14 and 18. The bargaining reading measured the seller's uncertainty, and read one of two quantities.** Merged into item 2 on 2026-11-04, since one instrument settles all three.
- **21. Agency was assumed where it could be derived.** Settled on 2026-11-04. [05-governance-forms.md](./05-governance-forms.md) section 5 derives the stakes from Axiom III with the decision roles as parties. The seller sinks on its representative's reading, a clawback is a hostage, a safe harbor is an allocated right and an expansion share is the stake at the next boundary, and the stake required is zero below the gate. A channel's stake follows from the fourth Friction Allocation Principle. The third standing assumption stays, narrowed to the premise that the parties are roles, which the bargaining cost needs anyway. The falsifier is in that section.
- **3. The costs were causally coupled and the coupling was unmodeled.** Settled in theory on 2026-11-05. Both couplings run from exposure into the bargaining count, and neither needs a coefficient. [02-mathematical-models.md](./02-mathematical-models.md) section 3.5 reads the count after exposure, adding each role a divergent step imposes on and each approver the buyer's rules require at that exposure, and names the loop back from coalition size to new requirements. The reading is dated, and a lower bound until fit is verified, so where the sale starts is a reading under Axiom III's gap. The cost floor corollary gains the policing floor a misrepresented fit lowers for the whole category, Akerlof's mechanism fed by discovery after sinking. The empirical half moved to the calibration list under the same number.
- **27. The gaps had drift rates and no equilibrium.** Settled in theory on 2026-11-05. [02-mathematical-models.md](./02-mathematical-models.md) section 5.3 splits drift into stale verification, held at $\gamma/(\gamma+\eta)$ by maintenance, and fit drifting apart, which re-verification cannot reach and notice of change, re-adaptation and an allocated change process govern. Section 5.7 makes account management the seller's maintenance, funded only while the margin at the next boundary covers keeping pace with the rebuild. Section 5.8 states separation as Spence's condition, crossed in both directions as entry pools a market and the premium pays to re-separate it, and splits the search cost's response to information by verifiability. The tests moved to the calibration list under the same number.
- **4. The division-of-labor corollary predicted an org design it did not describe.** Settled on 2026-11-05. [01-motions.md](./01-motions.md) section 9 keeps the buyer's stages fixed, awareness, consideration, purchase and renewal, and treats each cost the buyer cannot get through alone as a job staffed at the stage where it arises. Any cost, as the buyer expects it, can keep the stages from starting, and search is paid first and forms that expectation. The standard organization's error is one owner and one job per stage. Whether an owner travels is settled by discovery drift: early discovery decays whoever holds it, so the specialist works the proof and the sinking and hands off through the Blueprint between them. *Starts*, in where the sale starts, means the first seller investment. The falsifiers are in that section.
- **1. Why three costs and not four.** Settled on 2026-11-05. Exit cost fails the stated test: every seller investment that lowers it moves another cost. Migration is the buyer's investment against policing in the new deal, termination terms are bargaining with the incumbent, and what the buyer sank with the incumbent is its quasi-rent there under Axiom III. Contracting was folded in by 5.0. Constitution 6.0 dropped the paragraph that argued this beside Dahlman's reduction, so this entry is the record.
- **23. Axioms I and II had no figure.** Settled on 2026-11-05. `make_figures.py` now draws each cost's position between its own thresholds for Axiom I and the two conditions as a price window for Axiom II, where a discount moves along the window and verification widens it. Both are sampled from the model.
- **36. A market whose cost floor sits above the participation threshold.** Settled in theory on 2026-11-05. The cost floor corollary states the sign, buyers who name the problem and buy from no one at the same cost whichever seller they meet, and [01-motions.md](./01-motions.md) section 6 names what opens each cost's floor and who pays: a seller whose own share of the deals unlocked repays it, or else a regulator, a coalition or a platform. The prediction moved to the calibration list under the same number. Outside capital opening such a market is item 44.
- **29. The deal reading had no mandate variable.** Settled on 2026-11-06. The [Deal Triage Calculator](../../practice/deal-triage-calculator.md) 9.0 reads the mandate in Step 2b, a named sponsor and allocated capital, apart from the decision roles and again at every occupant change.
- **2. The bargaining cost had no instrument set, and its gap had no reading.** Settled on 2026-11-06. The [Bargaining Ledger](../../practice/bargaining-ledger.md) reads the gap as Axiom III defines it, the share of decision roles whose occupant has not both stated their exposure one to one and received the answer, from a private decision-role map. It reads incentive variance from the same map, scored on each role's measure rather than its statement, so both quantities come from one instrument without sharing one error. It pays the cost down through a shared arrangement record that names no role and, where the sale starts at bargaining, a safeguard inside the buyer whose bond is staging, not consent. Whether occupants will state reservations at the rate the gap needs waits on data as item 28.
- **46. The standing review had a claim and no specification.** Settled for the specification on 2026-11-09. The [Sustaining Adoption Review](../../practice/adoption-review.md) section 2 derives it from the two drift sources. It reads stale evidence on the rows a change touched, fit drift as each new divergence and whose change opened it, and value against the signed outcome measure. Each finding invokes a right agreed in Stage, and a finding with no right is an allocation Stage missed. The cadence asked for here, read from $\gamma_k$, is not settled: the cycle is a chosen rule in [06-calibration.md](./06-calibration.md) section 3.3, and the rate stays calibration item 15. The Constitution's falsifier compares relationships with and without a review. Its treatment arm is a review agreed in Stage that reads both ledgers, pooled or dedicated, and invoked at least one right during the term. A review agreed and then lapsed belongs to the other arm.
- **24. Nothing is instrumented.** Closed on 2026-11-04. Section 4 of [06-calibration.md](./06-calibration.md) is that list, and it now carries the predictions that wait on data.
