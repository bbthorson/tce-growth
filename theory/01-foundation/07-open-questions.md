# Open Questions

**Version:** 2.0
**Purpose:** To record where the twelve claims of the [Constitution](./00-tcg-constitution.md) are under-developed. Each entry says what is missing and what would settle it. This register holds what writing or an instrument can settle. A question that only evidence can settle sits in [06-calibration.md](./06-calibration.md) under the number it was given here.

Item numbers are stable, because other files cite them, so a number is never reused. The last section lists the numbers that have left.

---

## Axiom I, the market

**6. The Market Reading has no worked case.** Claims 1 and 2 say a leader can read the cost in the way before any deal. Without that reading, the motion is chosen by imitation. *Settled by:* two real markets, chosen and read by someone other than the reading's author, with the cost in the way predicted before the first deals and compared with what the [Deal Triage Calculator](../../practice/deal-triage-calculator.md) reads on them. Then a book of deal readings checked against the market reading they sit under.

**5. Reachability has research and no measurement.** A buyer the seller cannot reach is the second blocker inside search. [channel-collapse.md](../02-research/channel-collapse.md) backs it, and the triage reads it as a yes or no. *Settled by:* a count-based reading of reachability that survives the same scrutiny as the other counts.

**34. A floor and a belief show the same sign, and no test tells them apart.** Claim 4 says part of each cost is set outside any deal. Buyers who only believe a cost is that high look the same: they name the problem and buy from no one. The [Market Reading](../../practice/00-market-reading.md) asks both questions in Step 4. *Settled by:* a market-level reading of each floor, taken apart from any deal and compared across deals in one market. A natural experiment exists in the home case. The 21st Century Cures Act required certified health record systems to offer a standard interface, and a public federal list records when each product was certified. That is a drop in a floor shared across deals. If claim 4 holds, hospitals' adoption of third-party applications should jump once their system's interface went live, by more than any single seller's spending moved it before. Nobody has checked that adoption data with the needed dates is public.

**44. Outside capital can hold a market's costs below what its floor supports.** Venture funding lets sellers carry a market's costs, search above all, at a loss no deal repays. A market can then look open, or look Turnkey, when it is neither. [01-motions.md](./01-motions.md) section 5 says who pays to lower a floor and is silent on spending that is never repaid. *Settled by:* a statement of what the Market Reading shows when the seller is funded from outside the market, and of whether the market stays open when the funding ends.

**49. Implementation holds work that economists file elsewhere.** The premise says implementation is wider than Dahlman's policing and enforcement. It includes the buyer's doubt about fit before the contract, which is information, and the labor of integration, which Williamson counts as a cost of production. *Settled by:* a statement of which part of implementation is a transaction cost, and why the parts belong under one name.

**56. The Market Reading counts missing work and not what the seller has to build.** Claim 5 rests on what a seller builds before its first deal. Step 5 of the reading names who does each piece of work today. That cannot tell work built once from work repeated in every deal, and it weighs a template the same as a field team. *Settled by:* a reading that separates the two and says how specific each is to this market.

## Axiom II, contracting

**45. Discovery spending should predict retention, and nothing records it.** Claims 8 and 9 say seller spending earns its cost when it shows the buyer what it will face, and that work skipped at Time1 returns at Time2. Testing that needs cost of sale split by what it bought, and no instrument records the split. *Settled by:* a cost-of-sale record that tags each activity as verification the buyer can check or as persuasion, by the [Friction Allocation Diagnostic](../../practice/friction-allocation-diagnostic.md)'s principles, read against net revenue retention. The test has to compare spending within each representative and segment, code it without sight of the outcome, and keep the deals that discovery disqualified in the count, since discovery raises retention by filtering alone.

**48. A rival can raise what this buyer expects a seller to cost.** In a competitive deal a rival seeds the request for proposal with requirements the seller fails, raises doubts about its integration, or forces a bake-off (Salop and Scheffman 1983). The Constitution moves what a buyer expects only through the category and the seller's own evidence. *Settled by:* a statement of whether a competitive deal is read once for each vendor, and of how a seller defends against a rival's claims.

**59. A sponsor with a stake is a party to the dispute.** Claim 7 has a sponsor with something riding on the outcome hold the arrangement together. A sponsor whose own measure rides on the initiative gains from overruling objections (Staw 1976, on escalating commitment). *Settled by:* a statement of what neutral judgment between roles inside the buyer requires, and who besides the sponsor can hold it.

**58. The Bargaining Ledger's bands saturate, and nothing tests them.** A coalition of nine or more reads High at the lowest variance, so the bands do not separate enterprise deals. Pass 7 of the trim drops the bands, and this entry leaves with them.

**57. The Enforcement Ledger routes on fractions that a few rows can flip.** On a deal with few rows, one verdict moves a half of the gap across a routing edge. Pass 7 of the trim drops the routing fractions, and this entry leaves with them.

## Axiom III, renewal

**62. Whether a buyer that built more stays longer on a failing fit.** Claim 10 says failure shows first as stalled use and contraction and only later as an exit. Switching-cost studies support the first half (Burnham, Frels and Mahajan 2003, Whitten and Wakefield 2006, in [tce-empirical-record.md](../02-research/tce-empirical-record.md)). The order is a hypothesis. Buyers also build more when they expect a better fit, which lowers churn with no lock-in at all. *Settled by:* account-level records of integrations built, against exit timing and contraction before exit.

**51. A failure after signature can always be explained afterward.** A verified deal that fails has three explanations: the evidence went stale, the fit moved, or the other side squeezed. Read afterward, any failure fits one of them. Discovery that finds a misfit also stops deals before signature, so the signed deals are already screened. The [Sustaining Adoption Review](../../practice/adoption-review.md) codes each finding by cause on the day it is found, though the account manager who keeps the record is paid on the renewal. *Settled by:* a book of those records read against churn and contraction, and a count of the deals that discovery stopped before signature.

**65. Whether account management teaches or only holds.** Claim 11 says the seller's knowledge of the buyer's environment holds only while the seller keeps it current. Proactive onboarding cut first-week churn and its effects decayed within a week, though accumulated usage stayed higher for months (Retana, Forman and Wu 2016). That fits knowledge going stale. A buyer that learns keeps the gain. *Settled by:* onboarding and support effects followed long enough to tell a decay back to baseline from a lasting shift.

**12. Nothing values what building would cost the buyer.** Claim 11 says a seller that takes more than building would cost teaches the buyer to build. The Adoption Review records the buyer's alternatives at renewal: a challenger it evaluated, its own team on the adjacent function, and its own build estimate. Nothing turns them into a number the seller can price under. *Settled by:* that number read from the recorded alternatives, and a book of renewals checking whether buyers build where it says they will.

**64. What holds a relationship sits in people as well as in workflows.** Claim 11 reads what the two sides built. Ties between the people on each side also hold relationships. Attachments between individuals weakened the link from a change in fit to switching auditors (Seabright, Levinthal and Fichman 1992), and managers' moves raise the chance that ties between their firms dissolve (Broschak 2004, from a secondary account). *Settled by:* a book of relationships coded for ties between individuals beside what was built, read against exits after a tie breaks.

**63. Whether contracts curb opportunism directly.** Claim 12 calls contract terms one layer beside trust and norms. Cao and Lumineau's meta-analysis (2015) finds the three jointly reduce opportunism, read at the abstract. A reviewer read it as finding almost no direct effect of contracts, which is unconfirmed at the primary. Contracts can also crowd out trust (Malhotra and Murnighan 2002). If either holds, the staged plan buys less than claim 12 says. *Settled by:* the meta-analysis's path estimates, read at the primary.

## Across the times

**66. The value reading is new and untested.** The Market Reading, in Step 2, and the Deal Triage Calculator, in Step 1, ask whether the buyer pays for the problem today before any cost is read. The triage also asks what the buyer has put into the purchase that it will not get back. Before them, weak demand passed as a cost, and a buyer that did not want the change was filed under search. Two statements are still owed. One separates a buyer with no people free from a purchase with no priority, which the triage's check on the buyer's people does not yet do. The other covers a new category, where no buyer pays for the problem yet and an unnamed category looks the same as no value. The proposed sign there is whether buyers begin paying anyone for the problem, rivals and internal builds included, once the category has been taught. *Settled by:* both statements, and the reading run on deals. On a stalled deal the test is whether the buyer puts in more once the cost it named falls. Raised on 2026-11-09. It is the writing half of calibration item 42.

**67. Two costs in the way are not ranked, because the costs share no scale.** The instruments return every cost in the way and rank none. A shared scale could be time: the time each cost takes the buyer, against how long its reason to act lasts, read from a dated trigger, a budget year-end or a deadline. A working session on 2026-11-09 pressed that reading, and four things followed. The three costs mostly run in sequence, so their times add, and on a path where times add the longest step has no special standing. Easing a cost gains at most that cost's share of the buyer's time. A cost acts as a wall only for work different people do in parallel and for what never finishes, such as a veto or a statutory minimum. And the seller has a window in the same unit, its runway. Time cannot read a veto, a wait the buyer chose because waiting informs it, a buyer with no dated reason to act, or what speed costs a sponsor in standing. *Settled by:* a statement of the window and its sources, and of which costs run in sequence and which in parallel. Then a seller's own pipeline records, days in each stage coded by cost and split into days worked and days waiting. If most of the time is waiting, a seller lowers consensus by removing steps more than by doing work. Two sources to check at the primary before citing: Djankov, La Porta, Lopez-de-Silanes and Shleifer (2002) measured the cost of starting a business in procedures, days and fees, and Liebman and Mahoney (2017) found lower-quality federal purchases under expiring budgets.

**47. An expansion is a new deal inside a relationship, and nothing reads it.** Axiom III asks what prevents churn and contraction, yet much of net revenue retention above 100 percent comes from expansion. An expansion has its own three costs: search near zero, implementation partly prepaid by what the buyer has already built, and consensus often reopened by a new budget owner. *Settled by:* a reading of an expansion's three costs, and a statement of whether what the buyer has built is the seller's leverage there or its exposure.
