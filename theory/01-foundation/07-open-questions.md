# Open Questions

**Version:** 1.3
**Purpose:** To record, in one place, where the theory is under-developed now that the axioms are fixed. Each entry says what the framework claims, what it lacks, and what would settle it. Entries are grouped by the axiom they weaken. The register holds what writing or an instrument can settle. A claim that only evidence can settle sits in [06-calibration.md](./06-calibration.md) section 4.1 under the number it was given here.

This register consolidates. Where a file already carries its own "what this does not settle" section, the entry here points at it rather than restating it. An entry leaves this file when the work is done or when the claim is withdrawn, and either exit is recorded in the closed list at the end. Item numbers are stable, because other files cite them, so a closed number is not reused. Closed and moved numbers are listed at the end.

## Axiom I, composition

**2. The bargaining cost has no instrument set, and its reading measures the wrong gap.** The framework measures the cost, the [Consensus Friction Calculator](../../practice/consensus-friction-calculator.md), and supplies nothing to pay it down. The incumbent practice, qualification frameworks and multithreading, lives outside the repository. This is the largest gap in the framework and [01-motions.md](./01-motions.md) says so. The reading has two faults of its own, formerly items 14 and 18. Axiom III says the bargaining gap is each decision role's uncertainty about its own outcome, and the [Deal Triage Calculator](../../practice/deal-triage-calculator.md) measures how many decision roles have a documented objective the seller can read, which is the seller's uncertainty about them. [02-mathematical-models.md](./02-mathematical-models.md) section 2.4 records the proxy. And incentive variance, which sets the size of today's bargaining cost, and the bargaining gap, which feeds the chance of future loss, enter different terms of the two conditions while the field instrument reads only one. *Settled by:* an instrument derived from Axiom III's per-decision-role reading of the bargaining gap, which maps each decision role's own exposure, supplies the evidence that resolves it, and reads both quantities. [08-from-axioms-to-instruments.md](./08-from-axioms-to-instruments.md) section 2.2 states the gap as the share of decision roles whose occupant has stated their own exposure, and sections 4 and 5 derive what the instrument must do and the decision-role ledger it runs on. The instrument itself is still owed.

**5. Reachability has research and no measurement.** The search cost's second blocker, a buyer the seller cannot reach, is backed by [channel-collapse.md](../02-research/channel-collapse.md) and read in the [Deal Triage Calculator](../../practice/deal-triage-calculator.md) as a yes or no: a missing channel puts search in the keeps-out zone whatever the count. *Settled by:* a count-based reading of reachability that survives the same scrutiny as the other counts.

**6. The market reading has a draft and no worked case.** Axiom I's motion is chosen across a buyer population, and Axiom II's pursuit corollary decides whether to enter it, which are organizational decisions. Without a reading of the population, the choice of motion is made by imitation, the organization-level failure the Constitution names. [08-from-axioms-to-instruments.md](./08-from-axioms-to-instruments.md) section 2.1 derives what the reading must contain, and [The Market Reading](../../practice/00-market-reading.md) is its first draft: each cost's position across the population, where the sale starts, the exposure reading, the seller's cost to serve by cost, and pursue, restructure or decline. *Settled by:* a worked scenario on two anonymized EHR-integration populations, and a book of deal readings checked against the market reading they sit under.

## Axiom II, investment

Constitution 4.0 made investment an axiom of its own. Items 7 to 13 were filed here when Axiom II carried specificity, and they now sit under Axiom III with their numbers unchanged. Item 34, on reading a cost floor, sits under cross-cutting. Items 38 and 39, the two on file against Axiom II itself, wait on data and sit in the calibration list.

**44. Outside capital can hold a market's costs below what its floor supports.** Venture funding lets sellers invest against a market's costs, search above all, at a loss no deal repays, and so open a market whose floor sits above the participation threshold, or make one look Turnkey that is not. [01-motions.md](./01-motions.md) section 6 says who pays to open a market and is silent on investment that is never repaid. *Settled by:* a statement of what the market reading shows when the seller's condition is funded from outside it, and of whether the market stays open when the funding ends.

## Axiom III, future cost

**12. The unified-governance boundary has a condition and no instrument.** The fourth governance row says the buyer eventually builds it. [05-governance-forms.md](./05-governance-forms.md) section 3 now says when: once the buyer fails the bond condition, it builds when what the seller can take at a boundary exceeds the buyer's build margin, its own running cost plus the annualized build and migration plus any value its version would lack, less the price. Nothing reads the margin. *Settled by:* a build-alternative reading at the renewal, which is $V_{next\_best}$ made observable: the buyer's in-house team on the adjacent function, its own build estimate where it has one, and the bond reading from section 1. Its home is the renewal evidence pack in the [Sustaining Adoption Review](../../practice/implementation-motion/04-sustaining-adoption-review.md), when the practice layer is rebuilt for 5.0.

## Cross-cutting

**29. The deal reading has no mandate variable.** Implementation research finds that setting-level variables, sponsorship, capital and absorptive capacity, predict adoption better than individual characteristics, and a purchase stalls when the mandate dissolves whatever the decision roles say. The Constitution's definition of a decision role now separates an occupant change from the dissolution of a role when the mandate leaves with the person, and the framework reads the catalyst under the second standing assumption. Nothing reads whether the mandate still stands between them. *Settled by:* a mandate reading in the deal instrument, taken apart from the decision roles and re-taken at every occupant change.

**34. A cost floor has no reading apart from the deal's own cost, and the category prior has none at all.** Axiom II's cost floor corollary says part of each cost is set outside the deal, and the deal reading reports only each cost's position. Constitution 5.2 adds the category prior, what buyers believe each cost is before they meet any seller, and a floor above the participation threshold and a prior above it show the same sign. Telling them apart needs both read. The prior can be read from what buyers ask before discovery, the length of security questionnaires and requirement lists, and what analysts and peers say the category costs to buy and run. *Settled by:* a market-level reading of each cost floor, such as vendor count against buyer attention for search, the buyer's approval rules for bargaining and the attributability of outcomes for policing, taken apart from the deal and compared across deals in the same market. The review proposes a natural experiment in the home case. The 21st Century Cures Act required certified EHRs to offer a standardized API, and ONC's public Certified Health IT Product List (CHPL) records when each product was certified under 170.315(g)(10). That is a drop in the policing floor shared across deals. If the corollary holds, hospitals' adoption of third-party apps should jump once their EHR's API went live, by more than any single seller's spending moved it before. Nobody has checked that hospital-level adoption data with the needed dates is public.

## Closed since 4.0

- **8. The level conflated specificity with size.** The summed level retired, and specific exposure is now read on its own count, in Step 2 of the [Deal Triage Calculator](../../practice/deal-triage-calculator.md).
- **16. The coefficient $a$ crossed three boundaries by analogy.** It retired with the reduced form it sat in, and the case for verification over discounting now follows from price cancelling between the two conditions.
- **25. The equation stopped at signature.** The two conditions carry each party's expected future loss, its rebuild and its staging over gates, and recurrence enters through price and delivery cost summed over renewals. What remains of the time index is item 7.
- **26. The notation carried the field names.** 4.0 decided to keep the field subscripts, and the Constitution states why: the field names say what a seller experiences and Dahlman's say what the cost is.
- **13. The research file presented the decomposition as inherited.** [transaction-cost-economics.md](../02-research/transaction-cost-economics.md) now credits Coase with the question, Dahlman with the three costs and Williamson with the split by time, and names what the framework adds.
- **30. Whether the participation threshold rises with the value at stake.** Settled after 4.0, on 2026-10-09: it does not. The threshold marks the buyer's capacity to get through a cost, and value lives in the buyer's condition, where a bigger prize funds the investment that pulls a cost back under the threshold. [02-mathematical-models.md](./02-mathematical-models.md) section 6 carries the argument and its falsifier.
- **20. Value exogeneity is strong.** Settled by Constitution 5.0 on 2026-11-02. Value splits into realizable value, set by the offer and the buyer's situation, recognized value, the estimate the seller moves, and realized value, what the buyer captures. The boundary the entry asked for is between moving recognized value with evidence the buyer can check, which lowers the search cost, and moving it without narrowing the buyer's range, which is persuasion carried as Axiom III exposure. The objection that survives is item 32.
- **31. The Friction Efficiency Index rewarded work done before signature.** Settled on 2026-10-09: its target is now the allocation coverage ratio, the share of specific investment sunk only after an allocation covering it was agreed. The effort ratio stays as description, with no band and no weight.
- **9. Joint exposure had a form and no bond condition.** Settled on 2026-11-04. [02-mathematical-models.md](./02-mathematical-models.md) section 5.5 states it in the two quasi-rents: mutual exposure is a bond for a party while its share of the joint quasi-rent is covered by the other side's quasi-rent and what forcing a renegotiation would cost the other side. Hold-up enters $\pi_p$ as a second factor, and the imbalance, not the level, creates the hazard.
- **11. Frequency was three readings by convention.** Settled on 2026-11-04. [05-governance-forms.md](./05-governance-forms.md) section 1 reads them as regions of each party's stake at the next boundary, $F_p$ from the bond condition. One-shot is no stake on one side or both. Recurrent is a stake on both sides with the buyer still bonded. Continuous is the buyer failing the bond condition because its own quasi-rent has outgrown the seller's stake. The stake is named and not valued.
- **7. The allocation clause had no formula.** Settled in theory on 2026-11-04. [02-mathematical-models.md](./02-mathematical-models.md) section 5.6 derives the allocation required from the bond condition, which showed it rises with the imbalance between the two sunk investments rather than with specificity as such, and Constitution 5.1 restates the mechanism. The work done in advance has a placeholder form that falls with the share of misfit only use reveals. The randomized test moved to the calibration list under the same number.
- **10. Governance form and where the sale starts might interact.** Settled on 2026-11-04. [05-governance-forms.md](./05-governance-forms.md) section 2 says what each form safeguards and checks it against each cost's pair of parties. The forms govern the buyer and the seller, which is the policing pair. A deal whose sale starts at bargaining also needs a safeguard inside the buyer, a staked sponsor and an arrangement each exposed decision role ratifies, because the party that defects there is a role. Search leaves the form unchanged. The falsifier is in that section, and the instrument that reads the internal safeguard belongs to item 2.
- **14 and 18. The bargaining reading measured the seller's uncertainty, and read one of two quantities.** Merged into item 2 on 2026-11-04, since one instrument settles all three.
- **21. Agency was assumed where it could be derived.** Settled on 2026-11-04. [05-governance-forms.md](./05-governance-forms.md) section 5 derives the stakes from Axiom III with the decision roles as parties. The seller sinks on its representative's reading, a clawback is a hostage, a safe harbor is an allocated right and an expansion share is the stake at the next boundary, and the stake required is zero below the gate. A channel's stake follows from the fourth Friction Allocation Principle. The third standing assumption stays, narrowed to the premise that the parties are roles, which the bargaining cost needs anyway. The falsifier is in that section.
- **3. The costs were causally coupled and the coupling was unmodeled.** Settled in theory on 2026-11-05. Both couplings run from exposure into the bargaining count, and neither needs a coefficient. [02-mathematical-models.md](./02-mathematical-models.md) section 3.5 reads the count after exposure, adding each role a divergent step imposes on and each approver the buyer's rules require at that exposure, and names the loop back from coalition size to new requirements. The reading is dated, and a lower bound until fit is verified, so where the sale starts is a reading under Axiom III's gap. The cost floor corollary gains the policing floor a misrepresented fit lowers for the whole category, Akerlof's mechanism fed by discovery after sinking. The empirical half moved to the calibration list under the same number.
- **27. The gaps had drift rates and no equilibrium.** Settled in theory on 2026-11-05. [02-mathematical-models.md](./02-mathematical-models.md) section 5.3 splits drift into stale verification, held at $\gamma/(\gamma+\eta)$ by maintenance, and fit drifting apart, which re-verification cannot reach and notice of change, re-adaptation and an allocated change process govern. Section 5.7 makes account management the seller's maintenance, funded only while the margin at the next boundary covers keeping pace with the rebuild. Section 5.8 states separation as Spence's condition, crossed in both directions as entry pools a market and the premium pays to re-separate it, and splits the search cost's response to information by verifiability. The tests moved to the calibration list under the same number.
- **4. The division-of-labor corollary predicted an org design it did not describe.** Settled on 2026-11-05. [01-motions.md](./01-motions.md) section 9 keeps the buyer's stages fixed, awareness, consideration, purchase and renewal, and treats each cost the buyer cannot get through alone as a job staffed at the stage where it arises. Any cost, as the buyer expects it, can keep the stages from starting, and search is paid first and forms that expectation. The standard organization's error is one owner and one job per stage. Whether an owner travels is settled by discovery drift: early discovery decays whoever holds it, so the specialist works the proof and the sinking and hands off through the Blueprint between them. *Starts*, in where the sale starts, means the first seller investment. The falsifiers are in that section.
- **1. Why three costs and not four.** Settled on 2026-11-05. Exit cost fails the stated test: every seller investment that lowers it moves another cost. Migration is the buyer's investment against policing in the new deal, termination terms are bargaining with the incumbent, and what the buyer sank with the incumbent is its quasi-rent there under Axiom III. The Constitution says so beside Dahlman's reduction, and contracting was folded in by 5.0.
- **23. Axioms I and II had no figure.** Settled on 2026-11-05. `make_figures.py` now draws each cost's position between its own thresholds for Axiom I and the two conditions as a price window for Axiom II, where a discount moves along the window and verification widens it. Both are sampled from the model.
- **36. A market whose cost floor sits above the participation threshold.** Settled in theory on 2026-11-05. The cost floor corollary states the sign, buyers who name the problem and buy from no one at the same cost whichever seller they meet, and [01-motions.md](./01-motions.md) section 6 names what opens each cost's floor and who pays: a seller whose own share of the deals unlocked repays it, or else a regulator, a coalition or a platform. The prediction moved to the calibration list under the same number. Outside capital opening such a market is item 44.
- **24. Nothing is instrumented.** Closed on 2026-11-04. Section 4 of [06-calibration.md](./06-calibration.md) is that list, and it now carries the predictions that wait on data.
