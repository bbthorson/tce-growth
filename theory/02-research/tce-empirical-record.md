**Abstract.** This file records where the framework is weakest, on purpose. Three meta-analyses of the transaction cost economics record agree on a shape. Asset specificity predicts governance form reliably, across industries and methods. Uncertainty predicts it weakly and inconsistently, and high uncertainty often produces flexible relational contracting and joint problem-solving rather than formal safeguards. And specificity itself is usually measured with subjective, single-item scales, so construct validity is the record's standing problem. Two critiques sit beside the record. Ghoshal and Moran argue that governance designed around the assumption of opportunism produces the opportunism it assumes, and Williamson's reply is that economizing on transaction costs is about letting honest parties collaborate safely rather than about surveillance. List's field experiments show that market experience attenuates the endowment effect and loss aversion among experienced traders, which counts against borrowing an individual loss-aversion coefficient for organizational procurement. Farrell and Klemperer establish that contractual and data lock-in is real, measurable, and under-priced by buyers at signing. Choudhary's software economics attribute the adoption of subscription pricing to hosting economies, revenue smoothing and accounting treatment rather than to governance.

**Key claims:**
- Specificity predicts governance robustly. Uncertainty does not, and relational norms often substitute for formal safeguards under uncertainty.
- Transaction cost constructs are usually measured subjectively, and the record's explanatory power is weakest in services and relational exchange.
- Designing governance around opportunism can be self-fulfilling. Governance that lets honest parties collaborate safely is the defensible reading.
- Market experience attenuates behavioral anomalies, so an experienced procurement organization may sit nearer risk neutrality than an individual anchor implies.
- Lock-in is a measured phenomenon that buyers under-price at signing, and any account of a moat has to carry it alongside whatever else is claimed.
- Subscription pricing was adopted mainly for reasons other than governance.

**Where this bites in TCG:**
- **Axiom III, Law of Future Cost, the specificity half.** Supported for governance form only. The robust result is that specificity predicts which governance structure parties choose, mostly in make-or-buy and procurement settings. It does not establish that specificity raises the cost of transacting, which is why Constitution 4.0 reads specificity as exposure rather than as cost.
- **Axiom III, Law of Future Cost, the uncertainty half.** Exposed. The framework's uncertainty claim sits on the record's weakest result. The counting instruments were built against the measurement critique, and the account of when trust and joint problem-solving substitute for the instruments is not yet written. [07-open-questions.md](../01-foundation/07-open-questions.md) records both.
- **The third standing assumption.** Descriptive, not normative, and the Constitution now says so against Ghoshal and Moran. The decision-role ledger in [08-from-axioms-to-instruments.md](../01-foundation/08-from-axioms-to-instruments.md) records each occupant's reservation and the arrangement's answer to it, which is the cooperative reading built into an instrument.
- **The coefficient $a$, now retired.** List's results counted against borrowing an individual loss-aversion coefficient for it. Constitution 4.0 retired the coefficient with the reduced form it sat in ([02-mathematical-models.md](../01-foundation/02-mathematical-models.md) section 7). The results still bear on any later use of loss aversion in the framework.
- **The moat in [04-seller-surplus-model.md](../01-foundation/04-seller-surplus-model.md) section 7.2.** Farrell and Klemperer are why the information asset is stated alongside lock-in rather than instead of it.
- **The frequency corollary, twice corrected, now Axiom III's.** Choudhary is why the framework no longer claims subscription won for governance reasons. A second review then tested the 2.4 restatement, that the buyer's cost of exit at the next boundary was the variable underneath frequency, and it failed. Raising the buyer's outside option while the seller's specific investment is sunk tightens the seller's enforcement constraint and invites hold-up (MacLeod and Malcomson, Baker, Gibbons and Murphy). Contractual exit is a thin layer over procedural, technical and human switching cost (Burnham, Frels and Mahajan, Whitten and Wakefield). High-interdependence hosted software needs more formal safeguards, not fewer (Susarla, Barua and Whinston). Constitution 3.0 withdrew the restatement, and [05-governance-forms.md](../01-foundation/05-governance-forms.md) section 5 carries the frequency corollary with mutual sanction as its mechanism.
