**Abstract.** The review asked whether TCG is novel and whether it is sound.
- **Novelty:** the gate is new. Each cost has two thresholds and a position between them, and any one cost past its participation threshold keeps the buyer out. Also new are the gap between the cost a buyer expects and the cost it would actually meet, and the claim that Turnkey erodes as sellers enter. Most of the rest is published work recombined, and three claims understate their prior art.
- **Soundness:** six defects come before any data, among them units that contradict each other, a cost never linked to the investment against it, a headline rule that moves with chosen edges, and a falsifier that conditions on what happened after treatment.
- **The evidence:** it narrows every axiom and refutes none outright. Price shows up as a tax on surplus more often than as a wall. "Absorb the cost nearest its threshold first" is optimal only in a special case. Exposure raises the loss when a fit fails, not the chance of churn. And most of the restraint on opportunism runs through trust and norms rather than contract terms.
- **Overall:** TCG today is a vocabulary with three candidate theorems, none yet tested. Constitution 6.2 answers the first five defects, and 6.3 restates Axiom II's allocation rule.

**Key claims:**
- **The gate is the novel core,** and it is worth defending as such. Asking whether the buyer uses the market at all, rather than which governance form it chooses, is the cleanest statement of what TCG adds to transaction cost economics.
- **The costs and their thresholds have no common unit.** The theory states costs as fractions of annual contract value, and says the participation threshold measures a capacity that does not move with value. Both cannot hold.
- **"The sale starts at the largest position" depends on chosen edges.** Moving one edge reorders the costs. The rule is therefore not yet a claim about buyers.
- **The rule that the seller absorbs the cost nearest its threshold first is a special case.** The general rule is to spend where a dollar raises the chance of closing most.
- **Exposure raises the loss when a fit fails, not the probability of churn.** Buyer-side specific investment lowers voluntary churn in the switching-cost literature.
- **Contracts curb opportunism mostly through trust and norms.** Hostages and allocated rights are one layer of enforcement, not the main one.

**Supports in TCG:**
- **Axiom I — Law of Transaction Cost Composition** — a natural experiment on a procurement threshold supports cost floors set by statute and shows them working mostly as a tax on surplus. The non-compensatory rule is untested on transaction costs.
- **Axiom II — Law of Transaction Investment** — the economics of advertising backs the split between information and persuasion for measured effects, and refutes the claim that persuasion leaves the joint surplus unchanged.
- **Axiom III — Law of Future Cost** — the relational governance meta-analysis backs complementarity of contracts and norms, and narrows enforcement. Field experiments back seller-funded customer education.
- **Second standing assumption** — the complementary view of advertising presses open question 32 from a new side.

## 1. What is new

| Claim | Closest prior work | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| The motion is named after the cost nearest its threshold | Attewell (1992): one binding barrier, implementation know-how, sets how a technology is bought, and shared intermediaries lower it until buyers provide for themselves. Ram and Sheth (1989) pair each adoption barrier with its own remedy. Bunn (1993) tailors the selling approach to the buying situation. | **Recombination.** The formal rule, the largest normalized position, appears nowhere. Attewell is close enough that TCG should cite it as its nearest ancestor. |
| The costs are non-compensatory | Conjunctive screening (Gensch 1987, Gilbride and Allenby 2004, already cited). Veto players and non-fungible budgets supply the mechanism TCG names. | **Published in form, new in domain.** |
| Cost floors are set outside any deal | Williamson's shift parameters (cited), Aldrich and Fiol's shared legitimacy hurdle for a new industry, Attewell's intermediaries. | **Mostly published.** The per-cost floor tied to a participation threshold, and the split between floor and category prior, are TCG's own. |
| Seller spending earns its cost by raising value or by verification | Walton and McKersie's integrative and distributive bargaining, Bagwell's informative and persuasive advertising, Wernerfelt's sales assistance as matching kept honest by reputation. | **A restatement of standard economics.** Its contribution is the accounting test in open question 45. |
| Consensus-led has no established name | Schmidt, Adamson and Bird's "consensus sale" (already cited in [buyer-cost-neighbors.md](./buyer-cost-neighbors.md)), Gartner's consensus-creation job, Miller Heiman's buying influences. | **True of the label, false of the motion.** [01-motions.md](../01-foundation/01-motions.md) section 10 contradicts the repository's own citation. |

**What is new and worth defending:**
- **The gate:** two thresholds per cost, a position between them, and exclusion by any one cost.
- **Expected against actual position:** a seller can lower a cost and never show it, and the buyer never enters discovery. Hauser and Wernerfelt supply the mechanism, and applying it per transaction cost appears new.
- **Turnkey as a market condition** that erodes with entry, with sales-assist arriving as the vendor count rises at a constant buyer mix.
- **The seller's limit as directional:** the cost of sale is spent against a specific cost, so the addressable market belongs to what the seller is built to absorb.

**Where TCG misstates its prior art:**
- **The marketing TCE line.** [transaction-cost-economics.md](./transaction-cost-economics.md) says it "does not ask whether the buyer uses the market at all." Make-or-buy is that question. Rindfleisch and Heide review measurement costs before a contract and a scale of anticipated transaction costs. The accurate difference is that TCE's alternative to the market is hierarchy, and TCG's is no decision.
- **Coase's question.** The Constitution credits Coase with "whether to use the price mechanism at all," but Coase's alternative is the firm. The participation question descends from Akerlof and from search theory.
- **Gartner's buying jobs map nearly one to one onto Dahlman's three costs.** Gartner also finds the jobs run in no fixed order, which strains 01-motions section 9's claim that every buyer moves through the same stages.
- **The no-decision research.** Dixon and McKenna's remedy, taking risk off the table through limited and reversible commitments, is prior art for Axiom III's staging, and TCG cites the work only for its statistics.

## 2. Defects inside the theory

Each was checked against the files before any data, and the first five are answered in 6.2.

- **D1. The units contradicted each other.** Costs were fractions of contract value, while the participation threshold was a capacity that "does not move with value." A fixed capacity is a large fraction of a small contract and a small one of a large contract.
- **D2. A cost and the investment against it were never linked.** The gate read one quantity and the conditions another.
- **D3. The summed test was missing from the formal statement,** though the shared payer is the usual case.
- **D4. The headline rule depended on chosen edges.** Moving the consensus participation edge from 7 to 9 turns a position of 0.8 into 0.6 and can reorder the costs.
- **D5. Thresholds were read from exit.** [fear-of-failure.md](./fear-of-failure.md) called the no-decision rate "the participation threshold being crossed."
- **D6. One falsifier conditions on what happened after treatment.** Open question 46's treated arm is reviews that "invoked at least one right during the term," so a failing review can move into the control arm. The arm has to be assigned by whether a review was agreed in Stage. Not yet fixed.

## 3. The evidence, by axiom

### Axiom I

**Against.** When the US simplified acquisition threshold rose from $100,000 to $150,000 in 2011, purchases bunched under it, buyers cut scope to fit, and some purchases above it were dropped, in a model where buyers weigh red tape against surplus (Carril). Cutoffs tighten when information is more reliable (Huber and Klein), so a threshold and a floor share a driver. Buyers rate profiles containing levels they called unacceptable (Green, Krieger and Bansal), so thresholds cannot be elicited. More sellers do not reliably raise the buyer's burden: choice overload averages near zero (Scheibehenne et al.), high search cost protects incumbents (Honka), and a comparison site lowered prices by 8 to 15 percent rather than raising participation (Brown and Goolsbee).

**For.** The same threshold change moved participation at the margin with everything else fixed, which supports separate floors and statute as a bargaining floor. After GDPR, websites dropped about 15 percent of their technology vendors and concentration rose (Johnson, Shriver and Goldberg). Public buyers bunch under approval thresholds elsewhere too (Palguta and Pertold).

**What survives.** A floor set by rules works at both margins, and as a tax on surplus more often than as a wall. Non-compensation is untested on transaction costs. Thresholds depend on context, so a position is a reading under a stated information environment. Turnkey erodes where entrants' signals pool and no intermediary aggregates them.

### Axiom II

**Against.** If each threshold is uncertain, the first seller dollar goes where absorption efficiency times the hazard of the cutoff is largest. That is the cost nearest its threshold only when every cost is equally cheap to absorb and every threshold equally sharp, and it fails for a statutory floor, a sharper farther threshold, slack gates, a deal before discovery, or a cost every bidder shares. Allocation rules move profit more than budgets do (Mantrala, Sinha and Zoltners), and [01-motions.md](../01-foundation/01-motions.md) section 9 already says search is paid first. Persuasion changes the joint surplus in both directions, under the complementary view (Becker and Murphy, via Bagwell) and the persuasive one (Dixit and Norman, via Bagwell), so "the joint condition stays where it was" fails. Discounts and free trials bring in customers who retain worse (Lewis; Datta, Foubert and van Heerde), so price cancels inside a deal and not across a book. Enterprise software representatives conceded price to suit their commission plans at a cost of 6 to 8 percent of revenue (Larkin).

**For.** Measured advertising effects are mostly informative (Ackerberg, via Bagwell). Personal selling works best early in a product's life (Albers, Mantrala and Sridhar), and detailing mainly in the months after launch (Narayanan, Manchanda and Chintagunta). Transaction cost theory predicts the form of the sales force (Anderson).

**What survives.** The seller spends where a dollar raises the chance of closing most, weighted by its edge over rivals, and the motion names the binding constraint rather than an allocation rule. Spending that changes the decision without changing the buyer's evidence lowers retention where outcomes are verifiable. Absorption describes what sales work should be, not what representatives do.

### Axiom III

**Against.** Buyer-side specific investment lowers voluntary churn in the switching-cost literature, so "that exposure shows up as churn" has the wrong sign: a larger exposure raises the loss if the relationship fails, not the chance it fails. Contracts, trust and norms jointly reduce opportunism (Cao and Lumineau), and a reviewer read contracts as having almost no direct effect, which is unconfirmed at the primary. Contingency planning grows with repeated dealing (Argyres, Bercovitz and Mayer). Ties between people hold relationships (Seabright, Levinthal and Fichman; Broschak), and uncertainty rather than specificity moderates relational content (Cannon, Achrol and Gundlach). Retention spending aimed at the likeliest churners can fail, and should target lift (Ascarza).

**For.** Contracts and relational governance complement each other (Poppo and Zenger). Proactive onboarding halved first-week churn and raised usage, though the retention effect faded within a week (Retana, Forman and Wu). Firms with a dedicated alliance function report more successful alliances (Kale, Dyer and Singh), the nearest analogue to a standing review.

**What survives.** Specific investment raises the loss when a fit fails. The standing review helps under high uncertainty as a coordination device, and its invocable rights are untested. Hostages and allocated rights are one layer of enforcement beside trust and norms. Account management is funded where its lift times the margin covers its cost. Buyer failure, market-wide budget cuts, consolidation, acquisition and payment failure are scope conditions, and tests of the review need survival conditioning and time fixed effects.

## 4. What TCG predicts that its rivals do not

A theory that predicts nothing its rivals do not is a vocabulary. The review found three predictions that would make TCG a theory. None is tested.

1. **Price matters through approval thresholds.** It does not work as a smooth gradient in contract value. A low-price tool that triggers a security review or a finance sign-off stalls under self-serve unless a marketplace or a pre-approved vendor list carries the bargaining cost. "Sell to the contract-value band" predicts the reverse.
   - *Evidence so far:* public buyers bunch under approval thresholds (Carril; Palguta and Pertold). The off-diagonal cases are untested: low price with a high cost, or high price with a low one.
2. **Lowering a cost that does not bind does nearly nothing until the binding one falls.** Additive accounts of buying effort predict a gain from any reduction. *Evidence so far:* none on transaction costs.
3. **Risk remedies pay only above the gate.** Staging, guarantees and reversible commitments raise close rates where something specific is sunk, and add nothing where a trial verifies fit. The no-decision research recommends them everywhere. *Evidence so far:* none.

**Tests on public data:**
- **Prediction 1.** Re-estimate Carril's extensive margin split by purchase vehicle. A GSA Schedule order carries lower search and policing cost than an open-market buy, so a non-compensatory gate predicts equal deterrence in both. The 2018 and later threshold changes give further shocks.
- **The cost floor.** Join FedRAMP authorization dates to USAspending obligations by vendor, and run a staggered difference-in-differences on new agencies buying from each vendor. Authorization lowers a shared policing floor, so the corollary predicts more agencies entering, beyond a lower price.
- **Turnkey erosion.** Date the appearance of "contact sales" on archived SaaS pricing pages against category vendor counts, holding the target segment fixed by each plan's user limit.
- **The sign of exposure.** Regress public SaaS gross retention on the professional-services share of revenue, as a proxy for specific investment. The axiom as written predicts a negative sign. Switching-cost theory predicts a positive one.
- **Enforcement remedies.** In federal IT services contracts, compare terminations under award-fee and incentive-fee contracts (staged hostages) and option years (allocated renewal rights) against fixed-price and time-and-materials contracts. This extends open question 40.

## 5. The strongest objection

An industrial-organization economist said the conditions were not well defined, D1 to D3. An enterprise revenue chief said the instruments cost more to run than a team will spend before the theory is tested. A product-led-growth operator said existing heuristics already answer the self-serve funnel's decisions. The economist's ranks first, because ill-defined conditions make every test moot. The proposed answer, a threshold defined as a capacity of the party who carries the cost, read before the outcome, is what Constitution 6.2 adopted.

## 6. Proposed responses

Each is listed with the file it would change, and those applied say so.

1. **Restate the gate in capacity units** (section 5): the Constitution's Axiom I, [02-mathematical-models.md](../01-foundation/02-mathematical-models.md) sections 1 and 6, and calibration section 3.2. **Applied in Constitution 6.2 on 2026-11-09**, which also closed open question 50.
2. **State the identity between a cost and the investment against it, and add the summed test to Part II.** Defects D2 and D3. **Applied in 6.2.**
3. **Replace nearest-threshold-first with the general allocation rule** in Axiom II, keeping the motion as the name of the binding constraint. Withdraw the claim that persuasion leaves the joint condition unchanged. **Applied in 6.3**, with the general rule in [02-mathematical-models.md](../01-foundation/02-mathematical-models.md) section 1.5.
4. **Restate Axiom III's exposure as loss given failure,** and add the churn drivers above as scope conditions. Narrow the enforcement corollary to one layer beside trust and norms, and fund account management on lift times margin.
5. **Fix the falsifiers.** Assign open question 46's arm by intent to treat. Freeze the edges before any test of Axiom II's falsifier. Relabel the no-decision line in fear-of-failure.md, **applied in 6.2**.
6. **Correct the prior-art record.**
   - Cite Attewell, Ram and Sheth, Wernerfelt, Walton and McKersie, Hauser and Wernerfelt, and Gartner's buying jobs.
   - Withdraw "no established term" for consensus-led.
   - Fix the Coase attribution and the description of the marketing TCE line.
   - Credit Dixon and McKenna for staging remedies.
7. **File new open questions:**
   - the sign of exposure on churn;
   - whether contracts curb opportunism directly;
   - learning against rebuild in support effects;
   - funding by lift against margin;
   - specificity between people against workflow distance;
   - the general allocation rule against nearest-threshold-first, **filed as open question 61**.
