# Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research (CFIR)

**Sources:**
- Damschroder, L.J., Aron, D.C., Keith, R.E., Kirsh, S.R., Alexander, J.A., & Lowery, J.C. (2009). "Fostering implementation of health services research findings into practice: a consolidated framework for advancing implementation science." *Implementation Science*, 4:50.
- Damschroder, L.J. et al. (2022). "The updated Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research based on user feedback." *Implementation Science*, 17:75.
- Snow, D.A., Rochford, E.B., Worden, S.K., & Benford, R.D. (1986). "Frame Alignment Processes, Micromobilization, and Movement Participation." *American Sociological Review*, 51(4), 464–481.
- Benford, R.D. & Snow, D.A. (2000). "Framing Processes and Social Movements: An Overview and Assessment." *Annual Review of Sociology*, 26, 611–639.
- Klandermans, B. (1984). "Mobilization and Participation: Social-Psychological Expansions of Resource Mobilization Theory." *American Sociological Review*, 49(5), 583–600.

**Abstract.** CFIR is a meta-theoretical framework synthesizing 19 implementation theories into five domains: Intervention Characteristics, Outer Setting, Inner Setting, Characteristics of Individuals, and Process. Originally developed for healthcare implementation, it provides a diagnostic vocabulary for *why* an evidence-based intervention succeeds in one organization and fails in another. Applied to B2B sales, CFIR reframes resistance not as objections to overcome but as *implementation data* about structural, cultural, or psychological readiness. Frame Alignment Processes (Snow et al.) provide the tactical tools for moving stakeholders without persuasion-by-force.

**Key claims:**
- The same intervention can succeed or fail depending on context — implementation is not a property of the product alone.
- Inner Setting (structure, networks, culture, climate) determines fit and absorption capacity.
- Characteristics of Individuals (self-efficacy, organizational identification, motivation) explain resistance at the person level.
- Consensus Mobilization (agreement on problem and solution) must precede Action Mobilization (signature).
- Frame Alignment Processes — Bridging, Amplification, Extension, Transformation — neutralize resistance by aligning innovation with existing organizational values.
- "Resistance as a Resource" — vocal detractors expose real implementation constraints; objections are co-design opportunities.

**Supports in TCG:**
- **Axiom III — Law of Uncertainty Inflation** — CFIR's Inner Setting and Characteristics of Individuals are the structural sources of $F_{consensus}$ and $F_{implementation}$.
- **Friction Allocation Principles** — the Blueprint and Red Team operationalize CFIR diagnostic in the seller's hands.
- **Three Transaction Costs** — CFIR's process domain maps directly to the bargaining and enforcement costs.
