# Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research (CFIR)

**Sources:**
- Damschroder, L.J., Aron, D.C., Keith, R.E., Kirsh, S.R., Alexander, J.A., & Lowery, J.C. (2009). "Fostering implementation of health services research findings into practice: a consolidated framework for advancing implementation science." *Implementation Science*, 4:50.
- Damschroder, L.J. et al. (2022). "The updated Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research based on user feedback." *Implementation Science*, 17:75.
- Snow, D.A., Rochford, E.B., Worden, S.K., & Benford, R.D. (1986). "Frame Alignment Processes, Micromobilization, and Movement Participation." *American Sociological Review*, 51(4), 464–481.
- Benford, R.D. & Snow, D.A. (2000). "Framing Processes and Social Movements: An Overview and Assessment." *Annual Review of Sociology*, 26, 611–639.
- Klandermans, B. (1984). "Mobilization and Participation: Social-Psychological Expansions of Resource Mobilization Theory." *American Sociological Review*, 49(5), 583–600.

**Abstract.** CFIR is a meta-theoretical framework synthesizing 19 implementation theories into five domains: Intervention Characteristics, Outer Setting, Inner Setting, Characteristics of Individuals, and Process. Originally developed for healthcare implementation, it provides a diagnostic vocabulary for *why* an evidence-based intervention succeeds in one organization and fails in another. Applied to B2B sales, CFIR reframes resistance not as objections to overcome but as *implementation data* about structural, cultural, or psychological readiness. Frame Alignment Processes (Snow et al.) describe how organizers move what stakeholders believe.

**Key claims:**
- The same intervention can succeed or fail depending on context — implementation is not a property of the product alone.
- Inner Setting (structure, networks, culture, climate) determines fit and absorption capacity.
- Characteristics of Individuals (self-efficacy, organizational identification, motivation) explain resistance at the person level.
- Consensus Mobilization (agreement on problem and solution) must precede Action Mobilization (signature).
- Frame Alignment Processes — Bridging, Amplification, Extension, Transformation — align an innovation with values a stakeholder already holds.
- "Resistance as a Resource" — vocal detractors expose real implementation constraints; objections are co-design opportunities.

**Supports in TCG:**
- **Axiom III — Law of Future Cost** — CFIR's Inner Setting and Characteristics of Individuals are the structural sources of the bargaining and policing gaps: what each decision role cannot verify about its own outcome, and what the seller cannot see of the buyer's environment. Those two gaps decide the future loss.
- **The Enforcement and Bargaining Ledgers** — between them they read CFIR's Inner Setting and Characteristics of Individuals in the seller's hands.
- **Three Transaction Costs** — CFIR's process domain maps directly to two of Axiom I's costs, bargaining and decision, and policing and enforcement.
- The section below says where each construct is read in the instruments.

## Where each construct is read

CFIR names why an implementation succeeds in one organization and fails in another. The instruments read the same conditions as counts, rows and reservations. This table says which instrument carries each construct, so that editing an instrument does not silently drop one. Constructs no instrument reads are listed last.

| CFIR construct | Domain | Read in | As |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tension for Change | Inner Setting | [Enforcement Ledger](../../practice/enforcement-ledger.md), the deal record | The economic event, dated and sized in the buyer's own words |
| Networks and Communications, Structural Characteristics | Inner Setting | [Bargaining Ledger](../../practice/bargaining-ledger.md), the rows and the private map's "Reached by" column | The decision roles each step and each of the buyer's rules brings, and which roles the seller reaches only through the champion |
| Culture, Compatibility (with norms) | Inner Setting | Enforcement Ledger, Map | Rows marked "surfaces in use", filled by the [Sustaining Adoption Review](../../practice/adoption-review.md) |
| Implementation Climate, Learning-Centeredness | Inner Setting | Enforcement Ledger, Map | Previous attempts, and why each failed |
| Available Resources | Inner Setting | Enforcement Ledger, Map and Stage | The buyer invests first, and each gate names an owner on each side |
| Self-Efficacy, Knowledge and Beliefs, Individual Identification | Characteristics of Individuals | Bargaining Ledger, the private decision-role map | Each occupant's reservation, stated one to one, and the term that answers it |
| Compatibility (with workflow) | Intervention Characteristics | [Deal Triage Calculator](../../practice/deal-triage-calculator.md), Step 0b and Gate A | Workflow maturity, and the divergent steps counted when the product must fit an encoded workflow |
| Trialability | Intervention Characteristics | Deal Triage Calculator, Gate B | Whether the buyer can measure the gap itself and reverse the decision. Where it can, nothing specific is sunk and the Enforcement Ledger does not run. |
| Engaging | Process | Enforcement Ledger, Test | The skeptic, the person the integration lands on, judges every row |
| Planning, Executing | Process | Enforcement Ledger, Stage | Gates, each with a risk bearer and a priced right to stop |
| Reflecting and Evaluating | Process | Sustaining Adoption Review | The working review and the [RE-AIM](./re-aim-framework.md) value reading |

**Not read by any instrument.** *Relative Priority* has no row: the deal record dates the economic event, and nothing reads how fast its urgency decays. *Cosmopolitanism*, how far the buyer benchmarks against outside peers, has no row. Neither has a decision that turns on it yet, so neither is a gap to fill until one does.

**Frame alignment has no instrument.** Snow's four processes move what a stakeholder believes. Under the second standing assumption that moves recognized value, and the Bargaining Ledger answers a reservation with a term that changes the plan, the scope, the sequence or the money instead. [Open question 32](../01-foundation/06-calibration.md), now in calibration section 4.1, is the standing objection to that choice.
